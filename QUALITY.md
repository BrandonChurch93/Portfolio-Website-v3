# Quality bar

Read this before you design, build or judge anything a visitor will see. Read it again after a compaction.

`BRIEF.md` says what to build. This file says how good it has to be and how to tell.

## The bar in two questions

1. Would an engineer who builds this kind of thing stop scrolling and look closer?
2. Put next to Brandon's PC Builder, does it hold up, or does it look like the weaker project?

If either answer is no, it is not done.

## The patterns behind those verdicts

This list is Fable's reading of the table above. Brandon corrects it when it is wrong.

1. **Detail over count.** Fewer things, each with more to find.
2. **The real thing.** Real splash, real cars, real photos. Never a stand-in, never a placeholder shown as if it were finished.
3. **Light is physical.** It comes from somewhere (the cursor, the stream, a lamp) and changes what it lands on.
4. **Motion has weight.** Things speed up when they fall, settle when they land, and never slide or pop.
5. **Nothing boxed, cut off or cramped.** Pieces sit in the page. Openings use the whole screen. Space is generous and even.
6. **One light, one palette.** Everything sits with his portrait.
7. **Not a template.** If the layout could be anyone's portfolio with the colors changed, it fails.
8. **Made new.** A better version is rebuilt, not a draft with more added on top.
9. **Information stays.** Tags, descriptions and links are part of what makes a card good.
10. **Every screen is first class.** Phone, tablet, desktop and 4K. None is the lesser version.

## Six tests

Everything visual has to pass all six.

1. **Stop.** An engineer would stop and look closer.
2. **Lean in.** At three times zoom there is still detail. Nothing turns to mush or flat color.
3. **Lit.** It looks lit, not painted. Highlights and shadows agree with each other and with the light.
4. **Weight.** In motion it has mass and intent. Read the filmstrip left to right: it starts, builds, peaks and settles.
5. **Whole.** It belongs to the page: on palette, not in a box, not cut off, evenly spaced.
6. **Side by side.** Next to the PC Builder images it does not look like the weaker work.

## Fail on sight

- The stream as a glowing tube, a neon sign or wire, moving at one speed, or landing with no shape
- The stream looking pasted over something it is supposed to touch
- The stream reading as water, lava or fire
- Shards that read as confetti or paper: flat, evenly lit, all one size
- Shards that read as toys: faces all one tone, soft edges, rounded lumps where there should be points
- Shards buried in glare, or so many that the core is lost among them
- Shards bunched up where the stream lies along something or hooks round it
- A strike with no shards thrown, or thrown shards that do not bounce
- A glow with an edge, a glow that reads as a ball, or soft round spots of light
- Anything the stream throws heaping up where it lands, or knocking the stream off its line
- The stream reading as a mess: ragged, thrown about, not refined
- The stream racing. On the site it moves no faster than the visitor scrolls
- The stream turning where nothing makes it turn
- A post, rail or shelf for the stream that is a plain cylinder or bar, or that looks placed there only to steer it
- A plain box, cylinder or cone where a real object has form
- A window, screen or label that is a flat picture
- Anything cut off by the screen or by another element
- 3D sitting in a visible rectangle
- A color off the palette: yellow gold above all, and any white, blue or rainbow from the glass
- Cramped or uneven spacing, misaligned edges, a lone word on the last line of a heading
- Text that is hard to read over what is behind it
- A loop you can see repeating
- A placeholder ("picture to come") in anything shown for approval
- A phone or 4K view that is plainly the lesser one

## The stream

The core of light with shards that runs down the site. It is the most important thing on it, and the thing that has failed most often: three attempts at a stream were rejected before this build, and this build's molten metal reached 5.5 out of 10 with him. Read `BRIEF.md`, The stream, first.

**The sketch he picked is the floor**

- Open the two stills of it listed at the top of this file, and the live sketch at `/lab/stream-core`. The left panel, A, is what he picked.
- The finished version has to look plainly better beside it, and keep what he picked: one steady core, many shards that belong to it, two kinds of shard mixed, sparks, a live leading end.
- `LOG.md`, the entry "A core of light with shards, two ways", says how the sketch is drawn, what failed on the way to it, and where it falls short.

**What it looks like**

- **The core** is light with depth: a pale hot center falling through hot and flame to ember. It is steady, and swells and thins slowly as it runs. It reads as light, never as a tube, a neon sign or wire.
- **The shards** are facets: sharp, jagged pieces with flat faces and three or four needle points, no two alike. The larger ones have a notch in their outline, like two splinters fused at an angle. They come in a range of sizes with most of them small and none oversized. They are abstract. Nobody has to be able to say what they are made of.
- **He picked this look from a sketch**: the right hand panel at `/lab/stream-facets`, still in `reference/studies/08-stream-facets.jpg`. The sketch settles the shape and nothing else. Its lighting and its bounce are stand-ins.
- **The detail is in the light, not on the surface.** What keeps a facet from reading as a toy is how it is lit: every face its own tone, edges crisp enough to catch a hairline of light, points that glint. No surface texture.
- **Two materials, one family of shapes.** Any facet can be solid or clear. A solid one is dark until a face turns to the light, and then that whole face flashes. A clear one lets the light through, glows inside and is brightest along its edges. The two are told apart by how they take the light, never by their shape.
- **They belong to the core.** They ride round it and it is their light. A shard in front of the core shows its dark side against it. A shard behind it shows its lit face.
- **Many, but one thing.** The eye reads the core first and then finds the shards. It never reads as a cloud of loose parts.
- **It makes light.** The page, the edges of what it passes and the things near it are lit by it.
- **Its colors** are the palette's light (ember, flame, hot, pale) and its metal (bronze, copper). The clear facets carry light only inside that warm range.
- **The leading end** is its brightest part: a hot point with a short crackle of arc and sparks. It is alive even when the page is still, and stronger the faster it is moving.
- **Sparks** fly, fall, cool from pale to ember and go out. Some bounce.
- **A strike** is the best thing in it. Where the stream hits something, facets blast off it, bounce and spill away as if it had really struck, with sparks among them, more of both the faster it arrives.

**The test shot, before anything else is built**

One shot, alone on a blank page at `/lab/stream`, on the sketch's route: down from the top, parting on a title, both sides round its ends, one side landing on a card, running along it and off its end. It follows the page's own scroll. It passes when all of these are true:

1. Paused on any frame and enlarged three times, a shard is a solid object: flat faces each lit as its own plane, crisp edges, a lit side. A solid one and a clear one take the light differently. Naming a material is not part of the test.
2. The core reads as light with depth.
3. Beside the sketch it is plainly the better picture. Beside the PC Builder images it is not the weaker work.
4. It lights what it passes, and every highlight agrees with where the light is.
5. The leading end is alive at rest, gains energy with the speed of the scroll, and settles when the scrolling stops.
6. Landings have shape and weight: shards are thrown, bounce and spill off the edge. Nothing heaps up, and nothing thrown knocks the core off its line. Where the stream lies along something or hooks round it, the shards are no thicker on the ground than on a straight run, and they do not bunch.
7. In motion things have weight. Nothing visibly loops. Scrolling back up draws it back cleanly. It never moves faster than the scroll.
8. Shards near the cursor flash and turn toward it.
9. Every color is on the palette.
10. `npm run perf` shows it smooth on his Mac, and the phone view, with its budget said out loud, is not plainly the lesser one.
11. With reduced motion it is a finished still that still follows the scroll.

**How to build it, as far as it has been thought through**

- Build it new, in `src/stream/`. Lift ideas from the sketch, not its code: its shards are flat by construction, and a better version built on top of an approved sketch is something he has already rejected once.
- Look before building. Find photographs and film of the real things each part borrows from: long exposure trails of light, rough cut gemstones and broken crystal, in solid and in clear, lit from one side and from behind, sparks from a grinder and from a sparkler, an electric arc. Write down what makes each one read as real. Do not work from memory of what they look like.
- Find out how the best real time work of this kind is done before choosing a method: many lit objects at once, clear ones that carry the light behind them, glow that neither bands nor boxes. Say in `LOG.md` which method you chose and why.
- What the sketch proved, and is worth keeping: everything that glows is drawn first as an amount of light and given its color once, through the palette's ramp. Overlapping light is joined by keeping the larger. Faint light fades all the way to nothing. A shard's color comes only from how it is lit.
- Where the stream goes is planned, because later the layout needs it to hit exact places. What the eye checks (how a thrown shard flies, bounces and spills) is real motion with weight, stepped from the one clock with a seed.
- On the site, the things it lights are ordinary page elements. Light the title and the card in a way that will still work when they are real headings and real cards.

**The stream through Work**

The first place the stream meets a whole section. `BRIEF.md`, "The stream through Work", has the route and what is and is not built. It passes when the test shot's eleven still hold on this page and all of these are true:

2. **The posts and the rail are objects.** Enlarged three times they have form and detail. They are in the buttons' metal, lit by the stream and by the cursor's light, and they sit in the page, not in a rectangle. They look as if they belong there whether or not the stream is touching them.

4. **What a strike throws** bounces off whatever is really there, the posts and the rail included, and nothing heaps up.
5. **It reads as one stream meeting things in turn.** Scrolled steadily from the title to the button, the contacts differ in strength with how hard the stream arrives. It never reads as a string of equal bursts, or as a mess.
6. **The two sides match in route and never in detail.**
7. **Scrolled back up,** it draws back cleanly through every stop.
8. **The test shot at `/lab/stream` is unchanged.** Stills of it from before and after agree.
9. **`npm run perf`** shows it smooth on his Mac with both sides running the whole section, and at an iPad's size. The budget for two full sides is said out loud.

## How to judge

- **Look at it.** Take a still with `npm run shot` and a filmstrip with `npm run strip` for anything that moves. Open every image. Never judge something you have not looked at.
- **Assume your first verdict is too kind.** Look for reasons to fail it.
- **Check phone and desktop at least.** Check tablet and 4K before calling a section done.
- **Know what drew the picture.** The scripts print `hardware` or `software`. Software pictures (the web chat's workspace) are fair for form, layout and color. They are not fair for motion, smoothness or speed. Brandon judges motion on his own machine.
- **If it still fails after several rounds of fixing, stop fixing the same way.** Research how the best work of that kind is done, then rebuild that part.

## How to show Brandon something

1. Say what it is and where to open it.
2. Say what is at the bar, and what is not and why.
3. Say exactly what he is approving, and what he is not.
4. Never say the final will be better. Either it is at the bar, or you say plainly where it falls short.

## Why the second build missed

Keep these in mind. They are the mistakes this build exists to avoid.

- Rough drafts were approved on the promise that the final would be better. The promise was passed to the builder as a sentence.
- The page was never seen as one composition before it was built. Only parts were.
- The builder judged its own work. Its last independent review failed the hero and every piece, and it passed itself anyway.
- Single remarks were written down as permanent rules.

## What made the PC Builder work

Worth knowing when the time comes to hand work to Opus.

- It was one contained object with a clear subject.
- The handoff included a working reference of that object, with its source code and screenshots, labelled as the floor to beat.
- The quality document gave tests and a fail list, and told the builder to assume its first verdict was too kind.
- Fresh reviewers who had not seen the work being made judged the screenshots.
