What each shows:

- `engine-turned-case-1.jpg`: the one to judge the light against. The gold case of a 1911 pocket watch, engine turned round its middle, under ordinary lamps. The lights lie on it as broad soft patches that blow out at their hearts. Between them a darker swirl crosses the case, and it reads as a shadow. The fine pattern shows everywhere, each cut with a lit side and a dark one, strongest where a patch falls off. Round it are polished stepped rings with thin hard lines of light, and at the middle a plain polished disc with hairline scratches.
- `engine-turned-dial-1.jpg`: a real engine turned watch dial, silver, shot flat under even light. How fine and how even a watchmaker's cut is: the pattern can be counted and nothing in it wavers. The rings of the chapter are turned bands, each a different cut. Its pattern is barleycorn, not rays.
- `rays-round-a-boss-1.jpg`: rays round a raised piece at the middle. The back of a gold hunting case from the 1840s: turned rays under dark enamel, fanning from a centre piece that is a domed half pearl in a dark cup, inside a ring, inside a scalloped rosette set with beads. The centre piece was the reference for the handle as first built, and it was the wrong one: he rejected that handle as part of a watch dial. The handle is a knob from antique furniture now, and is not judged against this. He judges it against two shop photographs of cabinet knobs, which are not in this repo.
