## Problem 1: the city is soft
Why, as far as Fable has read it (check it, with numbers): the world's canvas is very tall, about 1460 by 1520 units, and its pixel count is capped. On a desktop a 3D canvas gets at most the pixels a 1440 by 900 canvas has at the tier's ratio (`TIER_PIXEL_BUDGET_AREA`), so on a 1440 wide retina screen the world is drawn at about 1.5 device pixels to a CSS pixel where the screen has 2. On an iPad it is capped at `HANDHELD.world`, 1.8 million pixels, a little over one pixel to a CSS pixel on a screen that has two, because Safari ends the page for memory (about 150 MB of GPU for each million pixels of this stage). So the towers' windows and the gold's edges, the finest things on the site, are stretched.
First measure it: for a 1440 by 900 @2 desktop, 1920 by 1080 @1 and @2, 2560 by 1440 @1, 3840 by 2160 @1, an iPad at 1180 by 820 @2 and 1376 by 1032 @2 (with `?handheld=1`): the canvas's CSS size, its drawing buffer's size, the ratio between them, and a crop of the skyline at the screen's own pixels (a PNG still at the device's scale) so the softness can be seen.
Then make it sharp. The idea Fable asks you to weigh first, since it attacks the cause: MOST OF WHAT IS SOFT NEVER CHANGES. The view never moves and the moon never moves. The land and the rock are already drawn once and kept as pictures (`kept.ts`). The city, the pyramid, the spire, the trees and the skin change on the screen only by what the cursor's light does to polished metal (`GLINT` in `light.ts`), by any flicker of their own (find out which have one), and by the stream's light. So the unchanging picture of them could be drawn ONCE AT THE SCREEN'S FULL DENSITY (in tiles through the same stage if one target that large is too much) and shown sharp, with only what truly changes drawn live over it at the canvas's own lower density: for example as a plain 2D canvas or image under a transparent live canvas, or as a kept texture sampled at the screen's density by a final pass that is itself at the screen's density, whichever measures best. A 2D picture costs about 4 bytes a pixel. This stage costs about 150. Other ways are yours to weigh against it: simply raising the budget where the frame's time and the memory allow (measure both), drawing only the on screen band at full density (`setWindow` already limits what is drawn), sharpening.
Whatever you choose must keep: the cursor's glint on the world's metal and its shadows as they are, the core's breath on the rock, the stream's light, everything standing in front of and behind everything else exactly as now, no seam or step between what is kept and what is live (not one pixel of offset at any scroll or zoom, at 100 and 125 percent browser zoom and on a resize), the two helpings the world is made in, and the first screen showing no later than it does now.
