## What must change, most important first
1. NO BOARDS, NO GRAIN. The bedding you built is regular: parallel steps of nearly equal width with rounded bevels (the great left slab at 330..450 by 300..470 reads as a stack of planks and pulls the eye more than anything but the ball), concentric partings round the ball (tree rings), parallel streaks down the tall slabs at the right (sliced timber). Rock breaks along fractures that wander and cross. So:
    - Take the partings off the bowl altogether, and take every parallel streak off the tall right slabs.
    - A slab's face is broken into three to seven sub blocks of 10 to 30 units by fractures at different angles, none parallel to the next, each sub block tilted a few degrees its own way so each takes the light differently, one or two missing so there is a black notch. Arrises are worn round, with a conchoidal scoop here and there. This is the detail of a middle size that both reviewers and you said is missing.
    - Where bedding stays it is on a few blocks only, of very different spacing (a ratio of one to five at least on one block), never more than three steps seen side by side, and no rounded bevel on a step.
    - The left stack: one great leaning slab with an irregular broken outline, dimmed to the picture's level (0.127: it is 0.068 and too red now, but as a stack of bright boards it reads far brighter than that at the page's size).
2. THE PATTERN WRAPS THE BALL. Squint at picture 9: a lit oval of folded layers wrapped round the ball, with big black wedges in it (left of the ball at 520..560, and above it under the roof), dark rock both sides. Ours explodes away from the ball as shattered radiating sheets, and the ring is a continuous busy crumple with small slits. Fewer, bigger, thicker slabs in the ring, their long sides following the ball's curve, and deep black wedges between them at the picture's places.
3. THE ROOF. Not built in two rounds now, so it is first after the boards. A thick pale grey brown crust from 268 to about 320 with chunky lit broken top edges (the lip measures 0.089 against 0.191), over a black sawtooth underside at 335 to 345 seen in silhouette against the lit ring, with the two hanging lobes to about 400 down at 450..520 and 830..900 across. The same stone, greyed by the moon.
4. THE FLOOR. A dim brown rounded mass (its top 0.12, not 0.20), not a bright orange crust of plates, with the rock under it as ruled above.
5. BOTH TORN EDGES RAGGED. The right one is a near straight seam at about 1005 to 1015 across with a flat pale grey plane beside it: a straight edge and a flat wall are both on the fail list. The crust shows its thickness into the break. Take out the long dead straight diagonal edges in the right mass (900..1000 by 440..520) and the pale slivers along them, which break into dashes (common.md, thin lit edges).
6. EVERY STAIR STEPPED EDGE. There are at least four: left of the ball level with its middle, over the ball's top left, the bowl's upper left, and top centre in the form view. Trace block outlines at a finer step or smooth the boundary before meshing. Also the flat brown crescent along the ball's lower left rim, the square notch at its right, and the triangle facets on the bowl right of the ball and on the floor's lit faces.
7. WHERE ROCKS MEET there is soft shadow, not a crisp cut line and not a hard black slot: the concave crease distance you store is for this.
