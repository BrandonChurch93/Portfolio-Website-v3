Brandon's words: "What I want is just much more debris closer to the bottom of this image. I want it kind of just floating there, some of it moving up and down while spinning, other plate pieces are tidally locked, moving right and left and so on. Does this look like something that would be in a AAA video game? If not then it's not a good enough scene." It is one scene: pieces near each other share a motion, with differences inside it.

## The field is the original plan's: do not lose it
A debris field was planned for this world before anything else was built, and Brandon was shown it and wants it kept: "in the original plan there was a debris field attached to it... I want to make sure that debris field wasn't lost. We do in fact need a debris field under this object." It is here:
- scripts/world/debris-plan.json: every piece of the planned field, copied out of the study (src/hero-blockout/plan.ts, debris()). 1,397 pieces: 9 great plates, 380 drifting pieces (a ring of small ones round each plate, and twelve clumps lower down), 124 whirling pieces in three arms round the stream, and 884 tumbling chips and dust. For each: at (its place, in the world's units), r (its size), kind (plate, whirl, drift or tumble: how it moves), long and flat (its shape), family (the pieces it moves with) and, on two plates, land.
- reference/studies/65-the-world-rebuilt-to-picture-9-what-broke-away.jpg and 65-...-the-whole-page.jpg: the stills of that field he was shown. Open both. That is the composition: great pieces under and round the planet's bottom, arms of middling pieces curling round the stream, clumps lower down, dust everywhere, thinning toward the foot.
- How each kind moves, from the plan: plate: a great piece. It holds its place under the edge it broke from, rises and falls slowly, and keeps one face to the core. whirl: arms of middling pieces that go slowly round the stream, sinking as they go, each turning over. drift: a wide low cloud of small pieces that slides from side to side as one. tumble: chips falling away along the stream.
- His words for it are in BRIEF.md (search for "The debris is one scene" and "His answers on the gold roofs, the debris and the moon").

## What Fable wants built
1. Rock, of the planet's kind: broken slabs and blocks with broad clean faces, sharp chipped edges, a few secondary breaks, never faceted crystals, cobbles, clay or potatoes. Some of the great pieces are plates of the planet's layered rock. One or two show a curved face that was the planet's outer surface. Sizes from 120 units long to chips of 2, in a real spread (few great, some middling, many small, dust).
2. Lit as the world is: by the moon from behind and to the right (its lit side has a crisp edge of pale light and its own shadow side), and by the stream, whose light falls on the faces turned to it, warm, fading with distance (the stream is a line of light down x = 0, z = SOURCE's z, from the core's foot: light the pieces from it in your material, strongest within about 150 units). The core lights the two great boulders from above (lit() with core on). Pieces shade themselves: store how open each place of each model is.
3. Motion, slow and heavy, in families (kinds from the plan: plate: holds its place, rises and falls a little, keeps one face to the core. whirl: goes slowly round the stream, sinking, each turning over. drift: a cloud that slides from side to side as one. tumble: chips falling away along the stream). Nothing races. A great piece takes a minute to turn. With state.still true everything is held, and a held frame is always the same picture.
4. It must cost almost nothing: read "Memory in Safari" in CLAUDE.md. A buffer rewritten every frame costs Safari 100 MB or more, so do NOT update instance matrices each frame. Give each piece its resting place, axis, phase, family and size as instance attributes that never change, and work its motion out in the vertex shader from one time uniform and a handful of family uniforms. Twenty or so rock models made by your script (use the surface maker for the great ones), drawn many times. Budget: 400,000 triangles drawn, 3 shader programs, 6 draw calls, 8 MB of textures. The moon's shadow map is drawn once, so nothing that moves casts into it: castShadow false.
