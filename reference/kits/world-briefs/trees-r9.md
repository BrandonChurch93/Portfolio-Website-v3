## What must change, most important first
1. YOU LEAVE OUT TREES THAT WOULD BE SEEN. Round 8 drew 334 of 575, and where the plan had 66 trees in the two front bands right of the spire the page showed two thin ranks on bare paving. A tree is left out only when under a tenth of its crown would be seen from the fixed view, measured against crowns as you DRAW them. On the page, right of the spire, no paving shows between the buildings' feet and the lip: open r8-stack-1.jpg, the picture above and yours below, and make yours the picture's. Cost: 150,000 triangles drawn or under, the far model for whatever is mostly covered.
2. RUST, NOT TAN. On the same crop your darks are (59, 30, 11) against the picture's (61, 20, 6) and your top five percent (202, 146, 104) against (180, 126, 90): take the green out of the shade, hold the lit tops lower, and keep the spread of tones from tree to tree. The shade inside a crown is a saturated rust, never grey brown.
3. A WOOD, NOT A MODEL RAILWAY. Same size round crowns, evenly spaced, each on its trunk with paving between: vary the outline and the mass of neighbours, let crowns overlap by a third so no ground shows between two trees of a rank, and show far less trunk (thinner, a warm dark brown near the crown's shade, mostly hidden under the lowest masses, an eased flare: they read as tar stumps and mushrooms now).
4. THE LEAVES. At three times every crown is the same stamped almond leaf in rosettes, and the atlas's tile is legible: smaller leaves that differ in size and angle, and no repeat that can be found, worst on the pale crowns.
5. THE MOON IS BEHIND AND TO THE RIGHT, and the crowns are lit from the front and top: a rim on each crown's right and upper right edge, the front softer.
6. THE SHADE ON THE GROUND is a dead horizontal dark band at one height behind the front rank ("a staircase of ruled stripes"), and under the row behind the dunes one ruler straight black line. Each tree's shade falls away from the moon (to the visitor's left and toward them), takes its crown's outline and softens at its end.
7. Make your part again at the very end against the land as it is then (its builder is still at work) and say how many of the row's trees are drawn.
