## A new ruling from Brandon, and it comes first
He looked at the world on the page tonight and said: "I think the sand dunes should be taller."
- Now: twelve crescent dunes whose crests stand 4 to 15 units over the sand (DUNES in src/pieces/world/parts/land/field.ts). A low building beside them is 20 to 30 tall and a tree about 15, so at the page's size they read as ripples on a flat.
- To be: about twice that. The tallest crests about 30 units, most 16 to 24, the small ones between them 9 to 12. Taller means steeper too: do not let a dune's back grow so long that the desert becomes one ramp (PROFILE.back is 8.5 times the height: bring it down so the backs still fit between the dunes, and the dunes still stand apart with flat sand between). A tall barchan has a long curved knife crest, long horns, and a slip face that is a great dark crescent of shadow. That shadow is what he likes in shots/world/ref/dunes-he-likes.png: keep that picture open.
- WHAT FABLE SEES, with shots/world/land/r2-dunes-beside-he-likes.jpg open: his dunes are HILLS. Each stands up off the sand as a rounded mound about a quarter to a third as tall as it is wide, its lit back a convex swell with its own gradient (bright along the crest, deeper down its flank), its dark face a large crescent that is plainly the far side of a hill. Their crests break the desert's skyline: the top edge of the sand against the night is a line of humps, not a ruler. Yours are a flat plain with dark crescents let into it: there is no hill behind any of the shadows, so each shadow reads as a sticker and the skyline of the desert is dead flat. That is what "taller" means. Height against width is the number to hit: a dune 100 units wide stands 25 to 30 tall.
- Their places, and the desert's outline, stay picture 9's. Lay it over the picture every draft: npm run match -- --at 880,180,420,150 --out shots/world/land/r3-match-dunes.
- What stands on or beside the sand must still stand on it: the line of trees behind the dunes (placed.ts puts them on the plan's ground(), which is the layout's own older dunes(), not yours), and the buildings at the desert's edge. Check each, say in your report where the plan's ground and yours differ by more than 2 units, and seat the sand to them where you can.
- The slip faces must read as real hollows in shadow, never as dark stickers: in a still of the page tonight (shots/world/wk-hero-700.jpg) each one read as a flat dark teardrop pasted on pale sand, with a hard outline all round. A slip face is shaded by form: darkest under the brink, lifting toward its toe where the sand beyond bounces light back in, its outline sharp only along the crest and soft at the toe and the horns.

## What else must change, most important first
1. THE BACKS ARE HILLS. Lit sand is one flat value across the whole desert (luminance 157 to 180). Each back is a convex swell that brightens up to the brink, with a lit crest line, darker in its hollows, and the floor between dunes rolls. A stranger must see volume before he sees shadow.
2. THE SLIP FACES are a flat decal: one value, brush stroke grooves, a serrated row of pale and dark pixels along every toe, ragged dark tails off the horns. Clean every one of those at three times. Give each face a gradient: darkest under the brink, lifted toward its toe by the light the sand beyond bounces back, each horn tapered to a clean point.
3. THE DESERT ENDS LIKE A TRAY. A smooth hard arc with a dark band of one thickness under it, "a lid laid on the planet", and at the right end it overhangs the skin with a black slot and pale slivers. In the picture the sand rolls over the planet's shoulder and thins out down its side in a scalloped, weathered lip: sand lying in the hollows of the skin, the skin's stone showing between. Build that roll over (10 to 30 units down the side, ragged, never one thickness), and bring its color toward the picture's cream at the rim (green over red about 0.78, yours is 0.71). The skin's builder is closing his side up to the land this round: say exactly where your lower edge is so the two meet.
4. THE TURNED DOWN FRONT EDGE under the city's front strip, and the strip's own face, are blocky camouflage whose square texels show at three times, and the strip's outer curve is ruler smooth. Real broken stone that never shows a texel, and a ragged edge. Where the lip is bitten (LIP in layout.ts) it is the broken edge of a slab of paving over rock.
5. THE PAVING is one perfectly regular grid of identical grey mauve flags with blurry brown blotches. Vary the flags' sizes, tones and wear, by district: large worn slabs in the courts, small setts in the streets, a different stone before the spire.
6. THE RIPPLES are one wavelength of parallel lines at one contrast everywhere: corduroy. Vary wavelength and contrast by place, bend them round each dune, fade them near crests and in the lee.
7. WHERE SAND MEETS STONE at the city's edge is a soft diagonal blur. Sand drifts over the flags there: thin tongues of it in the joints and against the feet of walls, a clean but uneven line.
8. THE FRONT STRIP reads as dark taupe concrete where the picture has pale lit stone. Where the trees' shadows do not fall it is pale cream stone in moonlight.
9. COST ON AN IPAD. The desert's shader is heavy and has not been measured there: npm run perf -- "/lab/hero?ui=0&handheld=1" --cpu 4, and say what you see. If it is over a frame, give the handheld a cheaper path (fewer steps in the shadow ray, no secant search) that looks the same at its smaller canvas.
