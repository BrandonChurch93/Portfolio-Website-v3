## Where it stands
Round 1 scored 3.5. What it got right is the hard part and must be kept: every outline sits on picture 9's to a pixel or two (the lip, the hollow, the floor's ragged underside, the lowest masses, open air below), there are real holes with depth and walls with thickness, and the bright ring round the core falls off with distance. Fable has since fixed two things in the frame that hurt it: the rock no longer mirrors the night (that was the grey in your darks), and the page now draws rock=r by default. Take stills of it as it stands now before changing anything.

It fails as rock. Side by side it is "an embossed relief of the photograph" in "faceted clay or cut card": about 1,500 flat patches of one size, evenly lit tan from edge to edge, where the picture is a few dozen great leaning slabs, brilliantly lit where they face the core, with a dark frame of heavy masses round them and black between. Fix it in this order:

1. THE DARK FRAME. In the picture everything left of about 480 across and right of about 900, and the roof's front face, is dark: warm near black with a few lit edges. In yours those masses are lit tan. They are dark in the picture because they stand NEARER the viewer than the core (the core is at z 150) and face the viewer, so the core, which is behind them, cannot light their faces. Put them there: z 300 to 480, faces toward the viewer or turned away from the core. Check with the baker: the stored share of the core seen on those faces should be near nothing. The roof's front face likewise: it hangs from the lip, in front of the core, and is dark with a pale top edge. This one change gives the picture its large pattern of light: a bright cave in a dark frame.
2. GREAT SLABS, NOT PATCHES. Merge the facets into the picture's great slabs: about 40 to 60 of them, 80 to 200 of the picture's pixels long, each ONE plane with a real tilt (the lit ones lean in toward the core and take its light in full, a soft even gradient across each), each 15 to 40 units thick with its edge standing over black. Inside a great slab the picture's smaller faces become its secondary breaks: a step where a layer sheared, a corner knocked off, a notch. A hierarchy: a broad clean face, a few breaks on it, chips along its edges.
3. BLACK GAPS. Under the roof's jagged lower edge, between the wall slabs, behind the floor: real holes 100 units deep or more that no light reaches, so the roof, the walls and the floor read as separate masses one behind another, four or five layers from the lip to the back wall. Today the gaps are shallow and partly covered by the lean of the slab edges.
4. NO RULED LINE ANYWHERE. Break the perfect circle the rock makes round the core: it is the clearance (nothing nearer the core's middle than CORE.r + 16) cut as a smooth band. Keep the clearance in depth, but let slab edges cross the core's outline in the picture at varied depths behind it, as the picture's do, so no circle can be traced. Break the nearly straight torn edge at the right (about 1045 across, 330 to 560 down) and at the left, and the long straight lower edge of the left hanging mass (about 270, 610 to 330, 700): ragged, with stepped blocks and notches of 15 to 40 pixels, as the picture has them. Roof points hang to 400 down at 450 to 520 and at 830 to 900 across.
5. CRISP EDGES. Slab rims are shaded smooth, like tubes. Edges are creased: a hard line where two faces meet, with a small real chamfer on the great slabs' edges that catches the core's light as a thin bright line.
6. CLEAN IT. Wood grain streaks inside the gaps, slivers at T junctions, long straight hairline seams on the upper left wall, airbrushed smudges of shadow at vertices, and stones stuck to walls and to the roof's underside: all go. Small stones lie only on faces that point up, in the angles where slabs meet, each with its contact shadow.
7. THEN BETTER THAN THE PICTURE CLOSE UP: inside each great slab, low steps of strata along its own direction (a few, broad, never fine parallel lines), chipped edges, a few hairline breaks. At three times zoom there is more to find than the picture has, and the broad faces stay clean.
