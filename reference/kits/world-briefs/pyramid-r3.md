## What makes polished gold read as gold, which round 2 does not have
Read reference/kits/README.md, "The look": "Metal reads as metal by what it reflects: long sharp reflections that slide across it. A soft orange gradient is paint." Round 2's faces mirror only soft blobs of light, so they are a gradient. Give the gold structure to mirror and a surface to mirror it in:
1. LIGHT AND DARK WITH EDGES. Inside the lit face there must be a real fall in brightness with an edge to it: a broad bright field that ends, a richer deep gold beyond it. Value across the lit face should run from about 0.45 to 1, not 0.98 to 1. The brightest part is pale gold (255, 227, 194 at the most) and is perhaps a fifth of the face. Most of the face is a saturated gold (saturation 0.5 to 0.7).
2. THINGS TO MIRROR, which this world really has: the moon as a small hard disc with a soft light about it, the stars of the page's sky as a few pin sharp glints that sit still, the long low band of the city's glow, the dark ground. A polished face that mirrors a few sharp things among soft ones reads as metal at once. Keep them few and quiet: it must not look like a picture in a mirror.
3. A SURFACE. The casing is plates in level courses. Each plate is a hair different in tilt, so a sharp reflection breaks very slightly from plate to plate (this is what makes real plated metal look real) and a soft one shimmers. Each plate is a hair different in polish too. Hairline joints, darker, never lighter. At the page's size this is only a living quality in the sheen. At three times zoom it is plates. No grid of bricks at any size.
4. THE DARK FACE is deep satin gold: it mirrors the dark half of the night, the city's low warm glow along its foot and one long soft reflection up its height. About (70, 45, 18) at its darkest to (160, 112, 52) where it lifts, saturated, never brown card.
5. EDGES. The front arris and the right outline each carry one clean pale line. The capstone's reveal stays as it is: a crisp dark band with a lit lip. The few bright specks along the upper front arris must go.
6. THE CURSOR. The glint (lit's glint: true) should show: when the cursor's light moves, one small sharp highlight slides on the gold. If the frame's glint cannot reach these faces, say what it would need.
7. THE FOOT. The gold runs into the ground. The land is being built by another builder: leave the foot clean and say what the land must do to meet it.

## How to judge it
Not beside the picture's glare. Put it beside reference/bar/pc-builder-parts.jpg and ask which is the weaker work. Ask a stranger's question: is that gold? At the page's size on the hero itself (npm run shot -- "/lab/hero?ui=0" --t 6 --wait 3000) is it the most beautiful object in the first screen? At three times zoom is there more to find? Measure: hue 28 to 34, saturation's median over 0.45 on both faces, value falling to 0.5 or lower somewhere on the lit face, nothing whiter than pale.
