## What must change, most important first
1. ONE POLISHED SHEET. The lit face reads from across the room as a single plane of gold ablaze. The plates are there to be found, not to be seen first: hairline joints, a bright catch on a chamfer here and there, and a difference of tone between neighbours a QUARTER of what it is now or less, which changes slowly across many plates (the cladding's own slow warp over 10 to 20 plates) with only a few single plates a clear step off. Every tone cell is exactly one plate, and every plate in a course is the same height (course hairlines ran straight through blocks three and four courses tall).
2. THE BLAZE HAS A HEART AND A FALL. It is clipped flat now (red at 250 or more over 99 percent of the heart: a plateau) and caramel everywhere else, so it reads as peach and butterscotch. Leave headroom: a narrow core at pale gold that is seen to be brighter than what is round it, a wide mid body of saturated gold about (235, 170, 85), deepening to the edges. Remove the soft dark band that runs parallel to the right outline.
3. SOMETHING IS MIRRORED. Nothing a viewer can name is mirrored in the lit face, and moving the cursor changes nothing on it. One or two long, sharp reflections that cross many plates, break by a hair at each joint and SLIDE as the cursor moves: the bright line of the land's pale lip low on the face, and a tall thin streak from the cursor's light running up the slope. On the blaze the cursor's streak is paler than the blaze, and beside it a touch darker, so it is seen on both faces. Prove it with real frames (npm run live) with the pointer moving across.
4. THE DEEP FACE is chocolate brown: a flat (54, 31, 12) over its upper two thirds, and the cap's deep side a dead flat (50, 28, 11). Deep satin gold mirrors the night: a gradient from about (45, 25, 10) at the top to about (95, 58, 22) low down, a soft long reflection across it, redder in its darks, never olive.
5. THE MIRRORED CITY low on the deep face reads as an LED billboard, a barcode, a pixel bar glitch: crisp vertical dashes of a skyline that does not stand in front of the pyramid, cut square by each plate. Take the skyline out. That face looks at the trees and the land's pale strip before it, and at the night: mirror those, softly (the gold is polished, not a looking glass), as warm blurred shapes broken only by the joints.
6. IT DOES NOT SIT. No shadow and no contact darkening show on the land at its right or front foot in any still. The moon is behind and to the right, so its shadow falls toward the viewer and to the left: make it seen on the ground, and darken the ground in the angle where the foot course meets it. (The land's builder is rebuilding the dunes this round and will not get to it: do it from your side, as a shade the pyramid lays on whatever is under it, if the stage's shadow map does not carry it.)
7. THE CAP's deep side wants the same gradient as the body's, and its lit side stays the brightest thing on the pyramid.
