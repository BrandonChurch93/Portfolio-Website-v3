## What must change, most important first
1. THE PALE SIDE HAS DEPTH. The left half measures 97 to 99 against the picture's 78, and the flank left of the large crater 70 against 17 to 32: where the picture has one smooth deep shadow (a moat, the ground turning from the moon toward the tear), ours is a pale speckled smear. Make it shadowed relief, not noise. The small pits are angular black polygons and scratch streaks with few lit lips: remake them as round bowls of many sizes, each with a bright lip on the moon's side and a shadowed far wall, and give the picture's rimmed craters at (1105, 375), (1165, 525), (1100 to 1120, 490) and on the limb at (1225, 345) and (1255, 360). Between them the ground is the picture's eaten, pitted cream, warm, not a smooth taupe with dots. Take out the polygon walled pits, the pale triangular slivers, the black needles across the low crater's lit floor and the stretched parallel streaks along the lower limb.
2. THE CRUST IS STONE. The slab, its chunks and the line of blocks down both tears are smooth shaded low triangles, crumpled foil, with almost no contrast (luminance 79 to 114 against the picture's 33 to 158) and a lilac grey beside the orange rock. Faceted rock fails on sight. Fractured stone: broken planes of many sizes, crisp chipped edges, dark pits, a lit warm cream top and a dark front. It is the planet's one stone (stone() in light.ts), and the rock's builder is making the torn edges ragged this round, so the two must read as one break.
3. THE CRACKS ARE CLEFTS. They are flat cream amber ribbons of one width and one brightness, with a dark drop shadow outline, and the knot has a rippled flame shaped fringe and a spill that sits to its upper right. A cleft has two inner walls, one lit and one dark, a hard broken lip, heat that falls off across its width and varies along its length by how deep it is, and a faint warm wash on the rock right beside it. The knot's pool is centred on the junction and hugs it. Thin arms taper to nothing and are never one even line.
4. THE LIP UNDER THE SAND. The top of the pale side is a straight horizontal seam where orange sand sits on taupe crust with a jagged fringe, and the pale tear is a near vertical clean cut. The picture's is a thick rounded lip in one cream that waves down toward the tear, and the tear's edge is rounded into thick broken crust. (The land's builder owns the sand: say in your report exactly where your lip ends and what you need the sand to do there.)
5. THE LARGE CRATER'S FLOOR is smooth plastic with a soft inner shadow edge and a striped shadow map artifact at its foot: terraces or a slump, a crisp terminator, no stripes.
6. THE DARK SIDE. Its limb has a pale band of scaly flakes that shows as a dotted line at the page's size (common.md, thin lit edges): a continuous dim rim light or none. The dark face is a cool grey black (blue over red 0.45 against the picture's 0.34) with faint horizontal banding: warm it and take the banding out.
