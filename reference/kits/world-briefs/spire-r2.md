What must change, most important first:
1. THE OUTLINE. Round 1 is a stepped wedding cake with a bronze cone: four flat corniced boxes, a lantern box with a hole, a small stacked cone. Look at the picture's spire again (reference/concept/09-parts/09c-the-skyline.jpg, the tall dark one at the middle): it is a slender rocket. One continuous taper from a broad foot to a needle, made of full height upright fins that run past every setback, with sloped shoulders where each stage narrows, never a flat ledge with a box on it, and the needle growing straight out of the shaft. Redraw it that way. This is where the picture's outline wins: an outline is layout. Then make it finer than the picture inside that outline: the fins have depth and a fine profile, the bays between them are narrow, the shoulders carry formed finials (not plain cones: round 1 had identical cones that overlapped into doubled cones), and near the top the fins open into a slim lantern through which the night shows without reading as a box with a hole.
2. THE RING IS POLISHED GOLD. Round 1's reads as a thin copper hoop with a top face of muddy taupe (86, 73, 62) and engraved lozenges that glint as a dotted line at the page's size. Make it a band of METAL.gold with a visible flat top face and a fine profile, deep saturated gold where it mirrors little and one strong pale highlight where it turns to the moon (behind the world, to the viewer's right). The engraving is a hairline that shows only at three times zoom and cannot stipple below that.
3. THE LIGHT OF A REAL THING. Round 1 is lit flat from the front in a neutral grey: "the greyest, deadest thing on the skyline". The stone is warm (the picture's is about 49, 32, 20 in shade: a dark warm stone, and paler warm trim). The moon is behind it and to the right: every fin, shoulder and the needle carries a thin crisp rim of pale light on its right edge, and the faces turned to the viewer are seen by the night's soft light and by the city's own windows. Light from its windows lies on its fins. Soft shadow under every shoulder.
4. THE WINDOWS. Round 1 fills whole bays with one identical grid of square cells from foot to top, which stipples at --scale 1, and some portal panes are greyish white (163, 154, 144): every lit pane is warm (ember through flame to pale gold), never neutral. Light it as the picture does: narrow upright strips of light between the fins, with solid stone between, their rhythm changing from stage to stage, in clumps, more of them and warmer than on its neighbours. Each lit pane has depth behind it at three times zoom. Nothing stipples at the page's size: fade what is finer than a pixel to its own average.
5. Judge it on the hero itself as well, where it stands under his name: npm run shot -- "/lab/hero?ui=0" --t 6 --wait 3000 --out shots/world/spire/r2-hero.jpg. It should be the most elegant outline in the first screen.
