The ball of light, the lines of light under it, the land, the city, the cratered and cracked skin each side and the loose boulders under the planet are not this builder's.

Judge, in this order, and say which of these it fails:
1. ONE SOLID BODY. Is it a cave in solid rock, great masses that touch and lean on each other, or separate flakes, cards or plates with air between them? Count the places where black is a gap you can see through and not a shadow.
2. ONE LIGHT. Cover the ball with your thumb: can you still say where it is from any block you point at? Are faces toward it lit, brightest nearest, falling away smoothly? Are faces turned away dark with a dim warm bounce, and only recesses black? Sample at least eight places against the picture's same places and give both numbers.
3. CRACKS. Thin dark grooves on continuous lit faces, following each mass's own joints, or gaps, a net, or parallel lines like timber?
4. THE MATCH, feature by feature, in the picture's pixels off: the roof's lower edge and its two hanging lobes, the bowl behind the ball, the left slabs, the floor's top, front and underside, the great right slab, open air under the floor, the meeting with the skin each side.
5. COLOR. Anything pink, grey, tan, pale past (245, 190, 130), or bluer than it is red? Any quilted, tiled, woven or wood grain pattern in the stone?
6. AT THREE TIMES. More to find than the picture has, in the same character (chips, rubble on ledges, fine fractures), or stair steps, facets from the mesh, mush, clay?
7. BESIDE THE PICTURE AT THE PAGE'S SIZE: would a person have to look twice to say which is the picture? Beside the old sculpt: is it plainly better, at a glance, to someone not told what changed?
