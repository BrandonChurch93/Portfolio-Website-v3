## What must change, most important first
1. ONE TAN BAND. Stone, copper and gold all sit in one orange tan band of hue and saturation, so nothing reads as metal. The rule for this round: ONLY METAL AND LIGHT ARE SATURATED. Every stone comes down to a saturation of 0.15 to 0.35: pale limestone, buff, a greyer granite, a share of dark near black stone and glass. Copper, bronze and gold keep theirs, and so does the light in the windows. Then the gold reads as gold and the city's warmth comes from its light.
2. GOLD DOES NOT BLAZE. It is matte satin tan. Polished gold is a bright face beside a near black one. The obelisk's point (68 to 128 down): one face at the hot light's cap, pale gold, its brightest tenth near (254, 242, 201), the next face deep brown. The crown's tiers: values spread from near black to bright (the picture's 10th, 50th and 90th percentiles are 21, 69 and 188: yours are 5, 107 and 157, a flat middle). Work out where the moon's own highlight falls, as the pyramid's builder is doing this round: the moon is behind and to the right, the viewer in front, so a face whose normal is near the half way direction between them (about 0.68, 0.45, 0.58) carries the moon's reflection, and faces turned to the left mirror the dark. Use it: it is real, and it is why one face blazes.
3. THE SUN DISC at the page's size is a pale cream clock face clipped at the ceiling, mean (224, 193, 155), where the picture's is a deep gold coin (133, 85, 34). Its far form: a mid to deep gold field near (230, 185, 120) at its brightest, a darker carved ring, one highlight crescent on the side toward the moon. No white.
4. THE GOLD CAP at 969 across is a straight cone. The picture's is a flame: it swells to about 1.6 times its base a third of the way up, then draws in through an ogee to the needle, with a blaze on one face.
5. THE WINDOWS CRAWL. In real frames of /lab/hero the window pattern crawls as the page scrolls, and tall shafts are lit like noise, cell by cell. Filter the window map at the page's size so a room smaller than a pixel never flickers, and light rooms by floor and in clumps on the tall shafts too. Prove it with real frames at the page's own size (npm run live), scrolling slowly.
6. NO MOON RIM. The rows merge into one caramel mass. The moon is behind them: a crisp pale line along each tower's right edge and roofline (a real rim from the moon on the chamfer, as the spire has), so every silhouette stands off the one behind it.
7. TWINS. Two pairs of identical narrow pinnacled towers stand side by side (near 830 to 965 across in only:towers, and near 1300 to 1420 in close:crown). Check every pair of neighbours and give one of each pair another design. TOWERS[4] needs a set back top with a needle, not a domed lantern. The copper drums' bands are recessed grooves between proud stone, not proud discs.
8. WEIGHT. A first visit downloads 31.6 MB for this world, and yours is the largest part: towers-city.bin 5.9 MB and its window map 2.45 MB. Bring the two to 4 MB together. Ways, yours to choose: the view never moves, so a wall that can never face the visitor and casts no shadow that is seen can go (check the moon's shadows after: the stage draws them once). A window map that is mostly noise packs badly as PNG: store only what cannot be worked out from a seed in the shader, in one channel, or at half the size.
