## What must change, most important first
1. LIT FROM ONE SIDE, TOP TO FOOT. The capstone and the top third are lit the wrong way round: the cap's moon side measures 0.11 against 0.29 on its far side. The face toward the moon is the brighter one over its whole height, cap included, and the cap follows the body.
2. THE TWO FACES. Without the bright stripe they barely differ (dark 0.23, the lit face's night 0.34). Split them over their whole height: the dark face about 0.2, the lit face's night 0.4 to 0.45 with a gradient.
3. THE BRIGHT FIELD IS A PAINTED STRIPE: parallel sided, flat at the clamp, like a studio softbox, with two to three pixel stair steps at every plate that read as aliasing. Give it a falloff along and across it, one soft edge and one sharp, and a second, dimmer mirrored shape with an edge in the mid field. Make the step at each plate large enough to read as plates or drop it.
4. THE DARK FACE IS A SMOOTH GRADIENT ("paint") and reads olive or mustard: hue 34 to 35 at saturation 0.9 is yellower and more saturated than the world's other golds (the gold pointed tower is hue 32, saturation 0.62). Pull it to hue 31 to 33 and its darks to saturation 0.75 to 0.8, and break it into two or three long mirrored reflections with edges that the plate joints break.
5. THE LEFT OUTLINE melts into the towers behind it (0.36 against 0.38): give that outline a pale edge light so the pyramid's long side stands clear of the city.
6. TOO MANY LINES. Four or five near parallel lines run up the lit slope (the field's edges, the moon's streak, the cursor's line, the outline) and the thin ones read as scratches or creases. Keep one fine line (the moon's). Make the cursor's light a broader, soft ended highlight that visibly slides (at the page's size it changes only 255 pixels), unbroken within a course and kept off the arris. Vary the front arris line's brightness along its length so it does not read as a drawn stroke.
7. AT THE PAGE'S SIZE the hairline reveal disappears, so the cap no longer reads as a separate stone: it must still read at scale 1.
8. THE FOOT. The right base corner sits 11 to 14 pixels short and 6 high against the picture (473 across): check it. A tree crown stands over it: say where, in the picture's pixels, so the trees can clear it.
