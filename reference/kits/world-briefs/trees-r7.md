## What must change, most important first
1. A CROWN IS A BALL FIRST. At the page's size each crown reads as one compact rounded mass with a clean outline (small notches, no sprigs standing clear of it), and you can count the crowns in a row. Its light is ONE gradient over the whole crown: lit on its top and its moon side (the moon is at the upper right and behind: MOONWARD in layout.ts), falling to a dark underside and a dark side away from the moon, with a crisp bright rim on the moon side. Between two neighbouring crowns the gap falls to the darks (about 45, 19, 5). The sprigs, holes and leaf detail stay, INSIDE that outline and under that gradient, as what a visitor finds at three times. Never plainer close up than round 6: organised, not simplified.
2. CROWNS DIFFER FROM ONE ANOTHER, AS THE PICTURE'S DO. Open r6-stack-0.jpg: in the picture's belt one crown is pale cream, its neighbour deep brown, the next amber. Ours are all one rust. Give each tree its own tone across that range (placed.ts already hands every tree a tone, 0 to 1: use the whole of it, with about one in five pale, one in five deep), so the row reads as a row of individual trees.
3. NOTHING FLOATS. Reviewers found a detached crescent of five to eight leaves above crown after crown (a repeated motif of the atlas), single flecks, and C shaped flakes that read as paper confetti. Every outer cluster is tied to the crown's mass or cut. A leaf reads as a leaf (a pointed oval with a midrib's fold of light), not a curl of paper.
4. THE RIGHT HEIGHT. Between the pyramid and the spire (433 to 680 across) the picture has one low row whose crowns top out at 245 to 267 down. Ours top out at 212 to 250: the belt stands 10 to 30 picture pixels too high and hides the buildings' lit lower floors. placed.ts gives every tree a size. Measure what you draw for a size of s (its width, and its height from foot to top) and report it: it should be s wide and s tall, give or take a tenth. If yours is taller, bring it down. If after that the picture's crowns are larger or smaller than ours somewhere, that is Fable's placed.ts: say where and by how much, in picture pixels.
5. THE MOON ON THE LEAVES. Beside strongly moonlit buildings the crowns take almost no moon (r6-look-moon). Item 1's rim is the answer: a real share of the moon on the moon side of each crown, none on the other.
6. THE ROW BEHIND THE DUNES is in the plan now: twelve trees at z -230, x 428 to 550 (ROW in layout.ts). The land's builder is raising a shelf under them and opening a pass before them THIS ROUND, so the land will change under you: seat on it as you do, keep SEEN, make your part again at the very end, and report how many of the twelve are drawn and where their crowns come in the picture (they belong at 227 to 243 down).
7. THE COST OF THE LEAVES' DEPTH. Writing gl_FragDepth turns off early depth rejection for all the foliage, and nothing was measured. Measure: npm run perf -- "/lab/hero?ui=0" and with &handheld=1, each with the trees and without (?only= on /lab/piece/world, or a switch of your own), one after the other, three times. If it costs more than half a millisecond a frame at the page's size, keep the look by a cheaper way (a card that is a shallow dome of a few vertices, so cards cross along a curve with no depth written, or depth written only where it is needed).
8. THE TRUNK close up is a plain tube on a cut foot: bark in relief, and a flare that roots into the ground. Pale specks where a lit window shows through a hole in a crown: close or darken those holes.
9. WEIGHT. The atlas is 1.77 MB, up from 1.48. Bring it under 1.5 MB without loss at the size it is seen.
