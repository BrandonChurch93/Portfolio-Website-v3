## What must change, most important first
1. STONE THAT BROKE. A great piece is a block of the planet: a few large planar breaks meeting at sharp steps, with chipped corners, a hackled edge where it tore, and the planet's own stone on its faces (the rock's material: pits, grain, crumbs at ONE scale in every direction). Not a rounded pillow, not a crinkled sheet, not a ruler straight evenly lit facet. Find and remove: the regular grid of dimples on the lit sliver under the right great piece ("perforated metal"), the evenly sized round dents in the left boulder's strata, the vertical streaks where a projection is stretched on a face seen edge on, the bright specks scattered over dark faces (a pit cannot be brighter than the face it is in).
2. THE MIDDLING PIECES are toys: smooth one tone hulls pushed through each other with black inner triangles showing, some a glossy candy orange. Solid chipped blocks, each face varying in tone across itself, no two hulls intersecting, nothing glossy, nothing orange all over: a body near black, lit only on the faces turned to the core, the stream or the moon.
3. THE CARRIED SHARDS are confetti, which is on the fail list: flat one tone orange triangles of one shape. They are pieces of the planet riding the stream (his story: the stream's shards are the planet's pieces). Each has thickness, a dark body, one hot edge toward the stream, and a shape of its own. Fewer and better.
4. THE TWO BOULDERS. Left: a rugged broken flank where it has stacked book page plates, and a chipped lower outline where it has a smooth arc. Right: a near black mass whose jagged upper left crest BURNS in the core's light, and nothing else on it orange: no even glowing outline, no round soft pocks. It reads as amber glass now.
5. A RIM FROM THE MOON. Near black bodies on a near black ground lose their outlines: picture 9's great pieces and boulders carry a thin cool pale rim on the side toward the moon (MOONWARD in layout.ts: the visitor's upper right, and behind). Give them one, sharp, at least a unit and a half wide where it shows (see common.md on dashed edges).
6. THE FIELD RUNS OUT. On the real page the lower third is nearly empty: restore the plan's lower clumps, let the dust be seen further down so the field dissolves and does not stop, and make the arms round the stream read as curves on the screen (more, smaller pieces laid evenly along each).
