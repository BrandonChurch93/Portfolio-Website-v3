## What must change, most important first
1. THE HOLES AND THE SLASHES. Round, flat, dark holes of one size, evenly scattered: "Swiss cheese", "punched card". Short dark slashes all pointing one way: "birch bark", "knife nicks". A single wavy line on the roof face over the core: "a hair". Vertical wax drip streaks on steep faces. A vertical pinstripe about 1.5 pixels wide on shadowed faces (a period of about 9 pixels at three times: find whether it is the map aliasing at a grazing angle or shadow acne, and remove it). Concentric record groove arcs on the floor faces left of the beam and on the lower left mass. Take ALL of these out. What a broken sandstone face has: LAMINATION (thin, continuous, slightly wavy, broken dark bed lines in bands a few units apart, each bed a tone step of a few levels, with grit gathered in its lower edge and here and there a small stepped ledge with a lit lip), a few irregular spalls, and patches of short crack strokes of very different lengths. Fade every bed mark to nothing on a face within about 35 degrees of the bed plane (that is where the arcs came from).
2. FORM INSIDE A FACE. Every lit face is one even fill (a spread of 8 beside the core), so it reads as parchment. The sculptor is cutting each plate into knapped sub faces this round, which will give you facets. On top of that: darken toward creases and the recessed side over 5 to 20 units, from the stored openness or the stored distance to a crease, and let the grain's relief show under the core's grazing light.
3. MORE DETAIL THAN THE PICTURE, NOT LESS. Measured fine detail is 20 to 43 percent below picture 9's at the same size (pixel to pixel step 2.38 against 4.16 on the left wall). Put back dense detail of varied size, of the kinds in 1.
4. THE DARKS are soft blotches 30 to 60 pixels across that read as suede. An unlit face is near black with hard lit edges. And the hue: the picture's darks are a nearly neutral brown (red over blue 2.4), yours rust (4.3). The 28 to 32 degree hue bin is empty in every box of yours and holds 7 to 10 percent of the picture's: proud lit edges and dust are yellower.
5. THE MOONLIT ROOF came out "lavender grey crumpled paper" (77, 50, 34). The picture's is a thick, chipped, pale grey tan lip. Under the moon the stone is a pale warm grey tan, never lavender.
6. ONE PLANET. Seen whole, the hollow's red orange and the skin's grey tan "meet with no shared tone", as if the inside were painted another color. Carry the weathered crust's tone into the first 10 to 30 units of the break at the planet's outer surface and under the land (pale, dusty, greyer), giving way to fresh red brown stone deeper in.
7. WHAT BROKE AWAY must wear this stone too: its builder is told to take the body color (stone()) and your grain map through stoneMaps, without your strokes and beds. Make that easy: an option on stoneShader or stoneMaterial for "grain only", and prefix your stream uniform names so they cannot clash with a part's own. Say in your report exactly how the debris should call it.
8. WEIGHT. rockmat-grain.png is 3.5 MB, the largest picture the world ships. It is noise: work it out in the page from a seed when the world loads (a few tens of milliseconds, in slices, as files.ts loads), or ship it at a quarter of the bytes. Under 0.6 MB shipped.
