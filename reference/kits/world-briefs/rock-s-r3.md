## The five things
1. COLOR, IN THE STONE. Open s2-final-stone0-beside.jpg: with no stone the light alone gives the picture's golden tan. The stone then turns everything one saturated orange. Measured: 77 percent of the rock's pixels sit in one 5 degree bin of hue (20 to 25), median saturation 0.84, mid tones (104, 47, 16), blue over red about 0.1 to 0.14. The picture: hue spread over 15 to 35 degrees, median saturation 0.72, mid tones (83, 42, 19), blue over red 0.28 in the far mids and 0.39 in the brights, brights at green over red 0.66. Retune the stone for sculpt S (STONE.quietTint and its albedo, behind the sculpt's switch): brights a golden tan to cream, mids a warm brown, darks a deep brown that is never black orange. Let masses differ a little from one another (a few degrees of hue, a tenth in value), as the picture's do. Give the table of these numbers for the picture, round 2 and round 3.
2. THE ROOF OVER THE BALL. The picture's roof does not end in a plain dark arch: its lower edge hangs in jagged slabs whose undersides are lit tan by the ball. Build those lit hanging lobes at picture (470 to 520, 340 to 380) and along 560 to 820 over the ball, and keep dark what the picture keeps dark: (560, 360) is 14 in the picture and 113 in S, (920, 360 to 400) is 12 to 17 and S has 40 to 96. On the roof's front at the upper left the moon draws thin pale lines with square and V bends that read as steel brackets and as a wire net at three times: a moonlit step is at least a unit and a half wide and broken along its length, or it is not lit.
3. THE FLOOR. It is a bright, nearly level shelf of parallel strata, and a flat salmon plane sits behind the stream under the ball. Make it the picture's: a dark brown mass that slopes away and is broken into uneven blocks, lit only on a few edges and one short lip beside the ball. Picture against S: the lip beside the ball 51 against 130, (560, 545) and (800, 545) about 30 against 73 and 96. The masses hanging under the floor at the lower left are 39 in the picture and 12 in S: they catch a little of the ball's light on the faces turned up to it.
4. NO RHYTHM. The eye finds three patterns: the stair stack at the lower left of the ball (a staircase), the two plank slabs at the left and book 2 (timber), and the upright corrugations on the slab up and right of the ball (louvres). And one angle of lip repeats across the stair stack, the floor and the right plates. Rule: never more than three lips in a run that are parallel and evenly spaced. Vary each mass's angle, its spacing (a thick layer beside two thin ones), where its layers stop, and cross every stack with one or two fractures that offset the layers. The flat pale lips read as icing (the floor's lip is (250, 172, 116), paler than anything allowed): a lip is a broken edge with form, chipped, never a band.
5. THE RINGS AND THE CLOSE VIEW. Within 1.3 R, 48 percent of pixels are lit against the picture's 22: turn the plates nearest the ball more edge on, with dark recesses between, and cream only at the ball's two sides ((575, 470) should be about 126, S has 74). At 2.5 to 3.5 R the picture keeps lit rims on 9 percent and S has 1: give the outer masses thin lit rims on the edges that look back at the ball. At three times: the even pinhole pits read as stucco (vary their size and gather them, leave faces without any), mesh facets show on the lips right of the ball, a saw tooth runs along the roof's lower edge, and the join to the skin's grey crust at the left is bare.
