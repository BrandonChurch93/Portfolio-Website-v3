## What must change, most important first
1. THE DARK WEDGE READS AS A CAST SHADOW. Both reviewers: the wedge at the upper left of the lit face, with its ragged sheet by sheet edge, reads as a shadow thrown by the city or by the capstone, and the cap's lit side (the same slope, the same facing) sits in the glare right above it. Fable's ruling: the direction half way between the moon and the visitor is within a few degrees of that face's normal, so the WHOLE lit face, and the cap's lit side with it, is inside the moon's glare. Take the wedge out. Picture 9's whole face blazes, and so does ours.
2. THEN SHAPE THE GLARE, so it is not a flat fill (it is 0.95 to 0.97 over most of the face at three times, "caramel or sand beside the dunes"): a hot pole low and toward the right foot, near pale gold with its saturation held over 0.45 and nothing clipped, falling smoothly to a deep rich gold (value about 0.65 to 0.75, saturation over 0.6) at the apex and along the front arris. From across the room: the brightest, most precious metal on the first screen, and a different tone from the dunes.
3. A FEW REAL THINGS, SHARP, IN THE MIRROR. Nothing of the world is mirrored at rest, so it reads as gold paint ("a soft orange gradient is paint"). Low on the lit face, in its bottom tenth: the dunes' crest line as one crisp thin line with a paler band under it, where the reflected ray really meets the desert. On the deep face: take out the invented dark skyline (sheared parallelograms and a dashed orange line that read as a smudge at the page's size) and mirror what is really before that face: the trees at its foot as crisp dark shapes and, above them, a few of the low city's lit windows as small sharp lights. Few. Where the ray really goes.
4. THE TRIM IS PLASTIC. The foot course and the front arris are cream peach bands ((253, 210, 163) with an orange stripe, the arris at the palette's ceiling): a polished course with a thin pale lip and a darker gold body, and an arris that is a hairline of light, a pixel and a half wide where it is seen, with no stepped dark fringe on its deep side at the foot.
5. THE CAP is matte, one value with the face, with a brown smudge at its tip: its own sharper finish, a crisp pale lip over the reveal, one hot glint at the tip.
6. CLOSE UP both faces hold seams and nothing else: seams that catch a hairline of light on one lip, a faint tint from plate to plate (under 8 levels), faint polish lines along each sheet.
7. THE CURSOR'S LIGHT shows on both faces at once when the pointer is near the front arris: on the face the pointer is over, only.
