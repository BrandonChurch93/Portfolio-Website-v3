## Where it stands
Round 2 scored 4.5 and 5, and Fable, looking at r2-close-right.jpg, puts the stone itself higher than that: it reads as sandstone now. Kept: the color family and its red to green ratio on the picture's (no white, blue or green in 8.6 million pixels). No wood grain, no net of cracks, no rice, no repeat. Everything with a direction worked out in 3D from each slab's own beds. Hairline joints that taper and never meet. One program, one map, no frame cost.

## The sculpt is being rebuilt under you this round
The sculptor's round 3 replaces the thin sheets with thick blocks of stone in real 3D (briefs/rock-r3.md: read it). So the flat card faces, the ruled lit lines beside the core, the ruler straight seam on the left wall and the triangle edges on flat faces are all being dealt with there. What that means for you:
- public/world/rock-r.bin will change, perhaps several times, and its triangles will no longer be "slabs". DO NOT DEPEND ON A SLAB'S NUMBER BEING THERE. Read the file's meta as rock.ts does and be right in every case: a block's own number in light.w or not, the distance to the nearest crease in light.w (meta.edge 'light.w') or in bounce.w (meta.edge 'bounce.w') or nowhere. Where there is no number of the block's own, take your beds' direction and shift from the place itself (large, slow fields in 3D), so the stone still differs from mass to mass.
- The rock no longer draws the sculpt's `skin` mesh (the skin and the land have their own parts). Leave that as it is.
- Judge the stone, as before. Say what you need from the sculptor.

## What must change, most important first
1. NOT KRAFT PAPER. At three times every face reads as torn kraft paper or card: an even fine speckle and a sponge mottle, with no relief inside the face. Bring the mottle's contrast right down, and give broad lit faces what the picture's have: sparse dark pits, gouges and erosion pockets that hold real shadow (a floor that the core's light does not reach, a lit far wall, a pale lip), a few to a face, of very different sizes, gathered where water would have worked: along beds and toward a face's foot. Most of a face stays clean stone with fine grain.
2. ONE ORANGE. 97 percent of the lit right wall's pixels sit in one four degree band of hue (24 to 28), where the picture runs from 8 to 32. Tone must come from light, not from one orange: creases, the insides of pits and gouges, occluded places and shade go redder and deeper (hue 10 to 16), proud edges and dust slightly yellower and paler, the faces nearest the core toward peach tan, less saturated (the picture's lit faces are a desaturated peach tan, not paint).
3. FACES TURNED FROM THE CORE stay mid orange where the picture's go near black, so a mass reads as a band of mid tones and not as a solid. Measure your own share of that against ?stone=0 (the hot zone near the core, your chip and dust colors, anything you add that does not come through the light) and cut it. The fill on rock is 0.15 now.
4. THE STREAM'S LIGHT IS TOO STRONG ON THE ROCK. The floor at the beam is the brightest rock in the hollow, (214, 127, 62) against the picture's (80, 40, 18), and reads as orange paint on the real page too. Cap it: with the stream all there, the lit lip beside the beam is no brighter than the walls beside the core, about (150, 85, 40) at most, and it falls off within 60 to 80 units. It is still by uStream (x how much, nothing lit below y).
5. THE STROKES ARE DECALS. Soft smudged bands 25 pixels wide at three times, with an offset shadow, running unbroken over folds between faces, in an even parallel row, reading as claw scratches right of the core at the page's size. A stroke is a groove cut in: narrow, hard lipped, lit on its far wall, stopping or stepping at a block's edge and where the normal turns, never crossing a crease. Uneven spacing. None on the faces nearest the core.
6. THE DARK MASSES are smooth low poly blocks with half the picture's luminance spread and no grain that survives. Keep the grain's contrast in the darks (it is lost in them now), and give chipped edges a thin catch of bounce.
7. EDGES. A uniform thin pale lip on every slab is one more thing that says card. A chipped edge varies: wide here, a hairline there, bitten, missing for a stretch. Use the stored distance when the file has it (either place). Without it do less, not a uniform line.
8. DUST AND GRIT on what faces up is written but not seen from this view: make it seen on the floor's top and the ledges (the shelf under the core is being built as one thick lit ledge).
