## So the method changes in one way, and only one
Keep everything you measured (the trace, the slabs, their depths and tilts). STOP BUILDING A SURFACE. BUILD VOLUMES. The hollow is an assembly of thick blocks in real 3D, each a solid bounded by fracture planes, placed by your measurements, standing in front of a dark backing. Overhangs, gaps you can see into, sides with thickness and cast shadows then come from the geometry, by themselves.

scripts/world/lib/surface.mjs turns a field (a function that is negative inside rock) into a mesh that keeps sharp edges, and scripts/world/lib/rock.mjs (makeRock) runs it, bakes it and packs it: sculpts A and B used them. A union of convex blocks, each the inside of six to twelve planes, is such a field. Sculpts A and B failed because their plates were typed in by hand, flat and all one size, not because of the tool. scripts/world/parts/debris/torn.mjs is how the debris builder made great pieces that both his reviewers called torn rock (a main mass split into blocks along its own fractures, blocks moved, notches, steps, opened cracks, rubble clinging): read it and borrow from it freely, so the hollow and what broke away are plainly one rock. Use whatever construction gets the result. The result is what is judged.
