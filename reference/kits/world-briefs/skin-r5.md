## What must change, most important first
1. THE CRACKS ARE GLOWING WIRES. Both reviewers: one width from end to end, all lit to their tips, salmon (249, 187, 141), an even soft fringe that reads as a halo, a staircase outline from the map's texels, the thin branch under the knot in dashes. A crack is a CLEFT IN ROCK with light at the bottom of it:
   - cut it into the surface: two dark walls that fall away from broken lips, the lips chipped and uneven, the light seen down between them.
   - its width varies along its length (the hot arm three times a thin arm, each arm swelling and pinching), and every arm tapers to a hairline and then runs on as an UNLIT fissure past where the light dies.
   - the light is cream gold at its heart (about 255, 240, 195), amber on the walls, and it lights the lips and a little of the rock beside them by how they are turned, never as a fringe of even width.
   - no texel steps at three times: work the distance to an arm out from the arms themselves (they are a few dozen line segments) or store it as a distance, never as a mask.
2. THE TORN EDGES ARE STILL A STRIP AND A SMEAR, for the third round. Dark side below the slab: "a near vertical strip of hatched planks" on "a ruled near vertical straight seam". Pale side: "a streaked smear from the skirt quads" with "a regular sawtooth". Take the skirt out. Build the crust at both tears as REAL BLOCKS: eight to fourteen a side, of very different sizes (the largest five times the smallest), each a thick slab with a weathered pale top and a dark broken section under it, tilted, stepping down toward the hollow, two or three broken off and hanging. The line where skin ends is their ragged ends, never a line of your own. THE SLAB at the top of the dark side is the largest of them: a thick pale grey block (the picture's is about (67, 51, 40) where yours is an orange (103, 71, 46)) that overhangs the dark face, with a lit broken front, one hanging chunk, a hard shadow cast on the face below it, and none of the rock's blocks showing through.
3. THE LARGE CRATER is a quarter too narrow, because its outer rampart is missing: the picture is in shadow (luminance 17 to 30) from 1120 to 1145 across where yours is lit (77 to 89). Raise a broad rim that shades its own outer slope on the side away from the moon. Give it an inner wall with slumped terraces and a floor with something on it (yours is a featureless ellipse). The low crater must not read as a black hole with a flat card in it. The small craters: a crisp lit lip each, shapes that differ, smooth shaded walls (no flat faceted triangles at three times).
4. THE PALE SIDE'S LEFT HALF has no shadow masses: the picture's ground there falls into near black between the craters as the dome turns from the moon. Its lit half is 10 to 14 levels hot and a little blue (190, 146, 108 against 174, 136, 92): bring it down and warm it by weathering and light, not by a new stone. The lit limb right of the large crater shows stretched diagonal noise at three times: real pumice, the same scale in both directions.
5. FACETS. The creased tents on the ground round the large crater, and anything else that shows a flat triangle at three times.
6. THE DARK FACE is smooth lacquer at three times: strata, hairline fissures and grain to find up close, still near black from across the room. A stray curved orange ribbon shows through it beside the slab: find it and take it out.
7. THE JOINS. The land's builder is remaking the sand's rim over your pale side THIS ROUND (sand rolling over a rounded brink, lying as talus on your stone, no drips): carry a crisp lit lip of crust under it and look at the join at the end. A black bar at the pale side's torn corner: find whose it is (only:skin against the whole) and fix it or say whose.
8. THE ROCK'S LINE. You measured the pale torn line 15 picture pixels left of the picture's at rows 320 to 430: the rock's next round moves it. Make against the rock's file as it is at your end and say which.
9. WEIGHT. 3.90 MB. Bring it to 2.6 MB or under: with the cracks worked out from their own lines the map can be far smaller, and the mesh need be dense only where it is seen close.
