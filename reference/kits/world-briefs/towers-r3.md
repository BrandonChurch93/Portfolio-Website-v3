## What must change, most important first
1. THE CITY AT THE PAGE'S SIZE. This is what a visitor sees first, under Brandon's name: npm run shot -- "/lab/hero?ui=0" --t 6 --wait 3000. Its window fields read as "a noise shaped brown and black mottle" and "blocky camouflage" where the picture has warm walls with sparse, crisp, bright rooms. Fix it at its cause: when a pane is under about two pixels on the screen, the field fades to its own average wall (warm, a little lighter than the stone in shade), and only the brightest lit rooms remain, sparse and crisp, each a small warm light. Light fewer rooms, more brightly. Then check motion: record a slow scroll with npm run live at scale 1 and at 2 and look for shimmer.
2. THE CITY'S LIGHT. It is lit evenly from the front in a greyish stone (about 133, 118, 102, saturation 0.23). The moon is behind the city and to the viewer's right: every tower has a thin pale rim on its right edges and a darker left face, the faces toward the viewer are seen by the night's soft light and warmed by the windows across the street, and the walls are a warmer, deeper stone (toward 110, 80, 55 in that light). The city is warm and lit: it should glow as one thing against the dark.
3. THE ORDINARY BUILDINGS ARE BOXES. "About two thirds of the buildings are boxes covered edge to edge in one size of square window", with a flat roof and one plant room. Bring them to the stepped tower's standard, which is the standard for all: varied windows and reveals, spandrels and mullions, stepped crowns and terraced setbacks, cornices with depth, each design recognisable by its outline. Four near identical ringed copper drums stand together in the middle of the skyline: no two neighbours alike, and the ringed pair's rings sit half way up, as the picture's do, not at the head.
4. THE GOLD IS MATTE OR WASHED PALE. The crown's tiers are flat ochre, the disc is satin bronze, the obelisk's lit face is cream. Polished gold is deep and saturated where it mirrors the night and pales only in a narrow highlight: let it mirror the night with contrast from face to face, reeds with an even pitch whose lit and shaded flanks agree (they read as a barcode at four times), and a glint that follows the cursor (lit's glint: true reads the frame's uGlintAt). METAL.gold is now a few degrees truer.
