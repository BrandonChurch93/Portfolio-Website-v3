## What must change, most important first
1. THE RING IS NOT GOLD. On the real page at scale 1 its luminance runs 89 to 149 and peaks at 168, where the stepped tower's crown beside it has a median of 193 and peaks at 223: it reads as "a matte cardboard hoop". It is the SAME polished gold as the crown and the pyramid (METAL.gold, mirror finish): a deep, dark mirrored body (it mirrors the night and the city under it) and, along its upper rim on the moon's side, one crisp bright continuous highlight that still reads when the band is 3 to 5 pixels tall. The far half, behind the tower, is dimmer than the near half. At the page's size its brightest run must reach the crown's.
2. THE RING'S EDGES. Its outline still stair steps into teeth along the near arc and the right end at scale 1 and 2. The bead and the horizon line on its outer arris break into a row of diagonal ticks ("a rope") and dotted dashes. A few grooves show as partial arcs near its ends and read as scratches, and short dark ticks across the bead read as mesh seams. Everything on the ring that is thinner than a pixel fades to its mean (fwidth), every line that is kept is continuous all the way round, and the outline gets true coverage. Prove it in real frames at scale 1 and 2 (npm run live), cut to the ring.
3. THE STONE IS CHARCOAL. The fins measure (64, 49, 39) and (91, 73, 59), saturation 0.39, where picture 9's spire is a warm dark brown (saturation about 0.5) of the same family as the city round it. Warm it and saturate it toward the city's stones, darker than they are so it still leads by its form.
4. THE STONE HAS NOTHING ON IT. Flat faces with no fall of light up their height, coursing barely there, weathering invisible even at three times, a faint ruled square grid on the shaft and shoulders, the podium's joints drawn as isolated black dashes over a pale speckle. A moonlit fall up each stage, courses and beds that differ, stains under the set backs, no grid, joints as continuous fine lines. No dotted moon rim on the shoulders (common.md on lit slivers).
5. THE WINDOWS at scale 1 are "stacked orange bars, a ladder" and "an orange smear with horizontal streaks", and the upper louvre plaque reads as a bright bracket. Break lit floors into clumps of panes whose mullions hold at one pixel, lower the contrast of a whole lit floor, and filter the window map so the strip resolves into calm floor bands at the page's size.
6. THE FOOT. The court is a plain two step disc with no pattern seen from the view, and the podium doors are flat brown rectangles. Give the doors a frame with depth and panels, and the court a lit kerb and a pattern that reads from six degrees up, or a form that is not a plain cylinder.
7. COST. 91,368 triangles, up from 74,082: bring it back under 85,000.
