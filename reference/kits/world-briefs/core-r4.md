## What Brandon said, which is this round
"Don't forget the core has to tie in with our stream that we currently have. It has to look seamless, like the stream exists there and come from it." And: the picture gives a thing its place and size, and the look is to be much better than the picture's. So the ball stays where and how big it is, and stays the picture's ball at the page's size, and three things are added that the picture does not have.

Look at the site's stream first. It is not a plain line. It is a braided core of light (a hot pale spine with strands of orange weaving round it and a soft glow), with small jagged shards of dark copper and glass riding it, and sparks at its leading end. Open reference/studies/06-stream-core.jpg and 06-stream-core-moving.jpg, take a still of it as it is on the hero now (npm run shot -- "/lab/hero?ui=0" --scroll 1150 --t 8 --wait 2500 --out shots/world/core/r4-stream.jpg), and read src/stream/shards.ts (how its shards are made: jagged facets) and src/stream/shaders.ts (how its core and its shards are drawn) and src/stream/config.ts (its colors and widths). On the hero the page draws that stream over the world's canvas, starting at the ball's foot (SOURCE), and only as far down as the page has been scrolled.

1. THE NECK IS WHERE THE STREAM BEGINS. On a page (anywhere but /lab/piece, where a plain line still stands in under it) the real stream leaves from the ball's foot. Take stills and real frames of the hero as it is scrolled past the core (npm run shot -- "/lab/hero?ui=0" --scroll 500, 620, 760, 900, each with --t 6 --wait 3000, and npm run live: read scripts/live.mjs) and make the join seamless:
   - Before the page has been scrolled far enough for the stream to start, the neck must be whole by itself: it draws down from the ball and thins to a thread that fades out, like a drop about to fall, with nothing cut off.
   - Once the stream is there, the neck must be the stream's own beginning: the same width and the same light where they meet, the neck's smooth column loosening into the stream's strands, no step, no doubled line, no change of color. If the page's stream starts at the wrong place or the wrong width for that, say exactly what the page should change (src/hero/stage.ts and src/stream/route-hero.ts are Fable's: do not edit them).
2. THE BALL IS MADE OF WHAT THE STREAM CARRIES. At the page's size the face is the picture's fine grain, as now. At three times zoom a visitor should find that the grain is not blur: it is countless tiny facets with sharp straight sides, like the stream's shards seen from far off, each with its own brightness and warmth, with no outlines drawn round them and no dark flakes (round 1 did this with outlined cells and dark tan flakes and it read as terrazzo: it failed. Light on light, facets told apart by brightness and warmth alone, never by a drawn line). The step from the page's size to three times must be seamless too: no size at which it turns to mush.
3. SHARDS OF THE STREAM ROUND THE BALL. A few of the stream's own jagged shards, of dark copper and glass, turn slowly round the ball close to its surface, as moons do, on tilted paths, some passing in front of the bright face (dark against it, with a thin lit edge) and behind it (hidden), each turning over as it goes. Now and then one drifts down toward the foot and is drawn into the neck. Few and small: eight to fourteen, 3 to 9 units long, slow (a turn round the ball takes a minute or more). They must never read as clutter, dirt or debris on the face, and at the page's size the ball must still read as clear: if in doubt, fewer. They are what gives the ball depth, and what makes it plainly the stream's source. Make them the way the stream makes its shards (read its code and make the same shapes and the same look in your own files: do not import from src/stream/).
   - Animate by uniforms and attributes that never change, not by rewriting buffers. Held (state.still), they are where state.t puts them: a held frame is always the same picture.

## What must not be lost
Nothing whiter than pale gold. No haze, glow or halo outside the ball. The hard edge. The measured face, band and neck at the page's size. Cost: say what each addition costs.
