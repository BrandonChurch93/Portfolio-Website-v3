## What is wrong with the live rock, and why eight rounds did not mend it
The live rock is sculpt R, round 8 (scripts/world/parts/rock-r/, public/world/rock-r.bin). Reviewers scored it 4 to 5 in every round. Look at the two enlargements side by side:
- OURS IS CONFETTI. Hundreds of thin separate flakes with black air between them. THEIRS IS ONE SOLID BODY: about twenty great masses that touch, lean on and overlap one another. In the picture black is never a gap you can see through. It is shadow: the underside of the roof, the side of a slab turned from the ball, the wedge where one slab leans on the next.
- OURS HAS NO LIGHT TO READ. Patches of pale pink, flat tan and orange lie beside each other at random. THEIRS IS ONE LIGHT, THE BALL: every face turned to the ball is lit, brightest nearest it and falling away smoothly with distance and with the angle, every face turned from it is dark, and you can point at any block and say which side the ball is on.
- OUR CRACKS ARE THE GAPS BETWEEN FLAKES. THEIR CRACKS ARE THIN DARK LINES ON A CONTINUOUS LIT FACE: a narrow groove whose far wall the ball does not reach. They follow the rock's own joints, so on one slab they run nearly parallel and a second set crosses them, and they are what divides a great face into parts without breaking it up.
- Ours is pale pink nearest the ball and has a flat tan patch with a quilted pattern at the left. Theirs is one family of warm orange brown from end to end.
Fable's earlier briefs asked for "great leaning slabs with thin ragged edges, in layers with real black gaps". That direction is what made the confetti, and it is withdrawn. Thick, joined, solid.

## The method is yours. These are the requirements
1. A SOLID. Build the rock as a closed body (a field of distance, or solids joined and cut, whatever you choose) and take its surface. No plate, card, flake or sheet anywhere: every piece of rock a visitor sees has thickness behind it. Looked at from 25 degrees to either side (a check for you, the page never turns) it is still a cave in a rock, not a stage flat.
2. THE GREAT MASSES FIRST, AT THE PICTURE'S PLACES. Lay them out against the picture before any detail (npm run match is the first look of every draft). R is the ball's radius. Read each one's outline off the picture with node scripts/world/grid.mjs:
   - THE BACK WALL. Behind and round the ball, a bowl: six to eight broad plates that overlap like scales, each 0.6 to 1.2 R across, gently curved, facing the ball nearly square on. The brightest rock in the picture. Where one overlaps the next there is a step 4 to 10 units deep that throws a thin shadow line. No air between them.
   - THE ROOF. One thick overhang across the whole width under the land's lip. Its top is a paler grey brown crust with a chunky broken edge. Its front and underside are in shadow, near black, with a ragged lower edge that hangs lowest at about 10 o'clock and 2 o'clock from the ball. Straight over the ball the roof's underside catches the ball's light as an arch of lit rock.
   - THE LEFT WALL. Three or four great slabs 1.2 to 2 R tall that lean toward the ball like fallen books, each with one broad face to the ball (lit) and a thick side to the viewer's left (dark, 20 to 40 units of real thickness), with narrow black wedges of shadow where they lean on each other. Left of them the rock goes dark in big blocks with dim lit edges until it meets the skin.
   - THE FLOOR. One thick ledge under the ball about 3.5 R wide. Its top is lit bright beside the ball. Its front face is a mid brown with breaks that run along it. Its underside is ragged and hangs in three or four points, where the small streams leave. Under it is open air: the page's stream falls there, in front of the floor, and nothing may stand in its lane.
   - THE RIGHT WALL. Not the left in a mirror. One very large slab at 2 to 4 o'clock with a lit face and a long diagonal shadow across it thrown by the roof, then darker masses with a dim orange rim, becoming the cratered skin.
   - Where the rock meets the skin at each side and the land's lip above, keep sculpt R's outlines, so neither has to be made again.
3. ONE LIGHT, AND IT IS THE BALL. A ball that size is a wide light: its shadows have soft edges that sharpen where the thing casting them is close. Faces square to it near it about (235, 165, 100). At the back wall's outer edge about (170, 105, 55). A lit face far out about (120, 70, 35). A face turned away is never flat black: it holds a dim warm bounce (30 to 45) from the lit rock across from it, and only deep recesses and the roof's underside go to near black (under 12). Nothing in the hollow is paler than (245, 190, 130), nothing is pink, grey or tan, and blue over red stays under 0.55 on every lit face. Sample these and give the table beside the picture's own numbers at the same places. If the frame's light cannot reach them (LIGHT in layout.ts and light.ts are Fable's: ?corepale=0 shows the core's light before it was paled), say exactly what number is wrong and by how much, and stop short of faking it in the stone.
4. CRACKS THAT ARE CUT AND LIT. Each great mass has its own two directions of joint, 60 to 90 degrees apart. Two to five fractures follow them across each face: grooves 1 to 3 units wide and as deep, wandering a little, some dying out part way, some widening to a notch at an edge. They read because the ball lights one wall of the groove and not the other. No net of cracks, no two masses with the same directions, no parallel set of more than four.
5. BREAKS OF EVERY SIZE. Great masses (60 to 200 units), then breaks of 15 to 40 on their corners and edges (a corner knocked off, a block slipped down a step), then chips of 3 to 8 along every edge, then loose rubble lying on the floor's top and on ledges, then the stone's own fine grain. At the page's size it reads as the picture does. At three times it has more to find than the picture has, in the same character. Nothing smooth as clay, nothing faceted like a cut gem, no pattern that can be seen to repeat, no stair steps or saw teeth from the mesh.
6. THE STONE. One warm stone whose color comes from the light. Fine and quiet: the form and the light carry the picture, the stone only keeps a face from being plastic. No quilted, tiled, woven or wood grain pattern at any zoom.

## How to look
First, before you build anything: the two enlargements side by side, and write down in your report, in your own words, the twenty great masses you see in the picture and where each is.
Every draft, in this order, and open every image:
1. npm run match -- --q "rock=s" --at 240,250,900,400 --out shots/world/rock-s/s1-<draft>-match
2. npm run look -- hollow --q "rock=s" --out shots/world/rock-s/s1-<draft>-hollow.jpg, then npm run look -- beside reference/concept/09-parts/09a-the-hollow-and-the-core.jpg <that> --out shots/world/rock-s/s1-<draft>-beside.jpg
3. The same with ?stone=0 (form and light alone, no stone): if the form does not read as the picture's without its stone, the stone will not save it.
4. npm run look -- close:hollow --q "rock=s" --w 1800 --h 1200 at three times.
5. The real page: npm run shot -- "/lab/hero?ui=0&rock=s" --scroll 700 --t 6 --wait 12000, beside the same without rock=s.
