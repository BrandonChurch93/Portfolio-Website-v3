## What must change, most important first
1. THE WHOLE LIT FACE IS BRIGHT, AND NONE OF IT IS FLAT. Above the mirrored horizon the face is dark brown: it mirrors black sky. It mirrors the MOON's side of the sky, and the direction half way between the moon and the visitor is within a few degrees of that face's normal: lay the moon's broad lobe over the upper field so it glows a deep bright gold (about 0.55 to 0.8 in value), hottest toward the horizon line. Below the line the field is "a flat clipped plateau" (253, 221, 186 over 65 percent of the face, saturation 0.26: cream satin): bring its peak under the clip, raise its saturation over 0.45 for most of it, and put the mirrored desert IN it (the dune backs pale, their hollows a deeper gold, a darker band where the court and the land lie at the foot). True pale is a narrow band under the line and one glint. Then the face blazes as the picture's does and has something in it, which the picture's has not.
2. THE DEEP FACE MIRRORS WHAT IT CAN SEE. The two tall black towers and the black tree band are cut outs of things that face cannot see (it looks left and toward the visitor: the tall city stands behind it). Take them out. What it does see is the low buildings at the left end of the city with their lit windows, and the trees before its own foot: mirror those, soft, a little blurred by the plates' finish, stronger toward grazing (Fresnel), never black, with a horizon that agrees with the lit face's.
3. THE PLATES. About forty identical evenly spaced hairlines read as siding, and in the upper lit field as bright stripes at the page's size. Seams are DARK hairlines, not all one spacing, with vertical joints that differ from course to course, a few plates a hair proud or dented so the mirror breaks slightly across them, and wear on the arrises and the plinth's edge. Tone varies continuously within a plate and never in flat cells: the staggered brick grid of flat rectangles about 3 percent apart is still there at three times, on both faces. (Round 6's mosaic of toned plates read as a glitch: this is geometry and finish, not tone.)
4. THE CAP is duller than the face and its lit side is split by a ruler straight line. It is the brightest point of the pyramid: polished harder than the faces, its horizon the same wavy line, one hot glint at its tip.
5. HUE. One hue from the deepest shade to the palest reads as a grade laid over grey. Inside the palette: the highlights a few degrees toward cream, the deepest shade a few toward red.
6. THE CURSOR'S LIGHT is "a thin hairline that reads as a scratch" and "a lens flare star" where it crosses the horizon. A short, sharp, bright reflection of a lamp that slides over the plates as the cursor moves and breaks slightly at each seam: no line, no tick, no flare. world.uniforms.uPointer gives the cursor's place.
