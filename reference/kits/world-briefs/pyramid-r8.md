## THE BLAZE
1. The lit face is ABLAZE: pale gold over at least two thirds of its area, about (245, 208, 155) on average there, reaching the palette's pale at its brightest, and deepening to saturated gold only toward the edges furthest from the moon's reflection. From across the room it is the brightest large thing on the top of the world. It is still the moon's own reflection (keep the real lobe: widen and strengthen it, do not paint a field), and it still has a fall across it that the eye can follow, so it is not one flat clipped plateau: leave the brightest two or three levels for its heart.
2. THE DEEP FACE IS DEEP. Beside the blaze it goes down to a warm near black gold at its top, about (38, 22, 9), rising to about (95, 58, 22) low down, with ONE broad dim warm band across it that reads at the page's size: the city's glow and the land's pale strip, mirrored softly. The contrast between the two faces is the whole effect: the lit face eight to ten times the deep one.
3. THE CAP is the brightest thing on the pyramid, with no soft round spot in the middle of its face (that reads as a sphere's highlight, or plastic, and fails on sight): a raking edge of light along its upper slope and a sharp edged band.
4. A MIRROR, BY ONE EDGE. Both reviewers want something a viewer can name mirrored in the lit face, with a hard edge, or it reads as satin paint. One is enough, and it must not read as a scratch: low on the lit face, the land's lit edge and the pale ground before the pyramid mirrored as a band a little brighter and warmer than what is above it, with a crisp upper edge that bends by a hair at each joint.
5. NOT A TILED ROOF. At three times both faces read as running bond brick with dark grout, and at the page's size the courses show as ribbing. Long, unequal sheets. Long horizontal seams lead, most vertical joints fade out, and a joint catches the light as a fine bright line more often than it shows as a dark one.
6. THE CURSOR'S STREAK is bent by the warp and jogs at a joint, so it reads as a glowing crack or a fold in foil. Straight, sharp edged, across a flat face, seen from wherever the pointer is over the top of the world, not only when it is on the pyramid.
