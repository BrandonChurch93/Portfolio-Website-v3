## What must change, most important first
1. THE RING IS TWO MATERIALS. It is a pale cream band at the page's size (luminance 162 to 171, saturation 0.5: the palest gold in the world, "a halo") and a deep brown gold when drawn larger (68 to 71, saturation 0.71), because its look depends on how large it is drawn (gSize, RING.near, the hold and the body). ONE material at every size and every screen: the world's polished gold (METAL.gold), which is mostly a dark mirror of the night with narrow hot highlights. At the page's size its value sits at or a little under the stepped crown's (about 204, 143, 76) with real darks in it, and its brightest run is a thin line.
2. A BLADE, NOT A TUBE. The band is a third to a half thicker than picture 9's (8 to 12 pixels through the front arc where the picture's is about 5) and reads as a glowing tube. Thin it to 5 or 6 units, with a bright top arris over a darker underside, so its section reads.
3. THE MIRROR HAS SOMETHING IN IT. At three times it is brown satin with one soft stripe. In its top face, as sharp streaks: the tower's lit window strip where the front arc crosses the shaft, and a scatter of the city's lit windows along the near arc. And its light on the tower: where the ring crosses, a warm catch on the shaft and the fins that can be SEEN with the ring on and off (it measured 56 to 69 and could not be seen).
4. THE STONE IS LIT FLAT. Its front faces are one tone left to right and top to bottom. The moon is behind and to the right: a crisp moonlit rim on the right arrises and on the top of every setback, the left faces a step darker, a fall of light up each stage. At three times it is "an even pinkish beige with faint courses and nothing to find", "a resin model": granite fleck, block courses as crisp as the stepped tower's, chipped arrises, dirt in the reveals, a tone that differs a little from stage to stage.
5. THE WINDOWS. At the page's size the central strip shows as amber smears and lumps: crisp floor and bay units, so it is floors of windows and never a texture of light. At three times each pane is a flat graded square: a reveal shadow, a recess, glass that answers a little differently from pane to pane.
6. SMALL FAULTS. A notch in the inner rim of the ring's left lobe at seven times, faint parallel bands along its top face, a small grey stub behind the ring by the left shoulder. The gold frieze as a flat lit strip at three times.
7. THE COURT. Long smeared horizontal streaks and a dashed dark line under the kerb show even at the page's size. Find whose they are (draw the page with only:spire and with the land off): if they are yours, fix them. If they are the land's paving inside the court, say so plainly at the top of your report with a crop, and Fable will give it to the land's builder.
