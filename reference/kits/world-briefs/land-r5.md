## What must change, most important first
1. ONE DUNE, SEVEN TIMES. Both reviewers: "pointed cones (witch hats) with one shared profile", "a row of shark fins", "a row of tents", all one template, evenly spaced, all facing one way. A desert has a HIERARCHY. One great compound dune, the tallest, with smaller dunes riding its back. Crests that are long, curving and sinuous, that link to one another and fork. Horns of different lengths. Dunes of plainly different sizes (the largest three times the smallest), spaced unevenly, turned differently (within what keeps the lit side toward the moon). Open shots/world/ref/dunes-he-likes.png again: his dunes are crescents with CONCAVE hollows on their dark side (the horns reach round near black shade with a crisp outline), not the convex flank of a cone. At least the large front dunes open their hollow toward the visitor.
2. THE CAST SHADOWS ARE GREY STRIPES. They measure (137 to 181, 100 to 134, 67 to 90), about three times brighter than the slip faces beside them, at lit sand's own chroma. A reviewer: "this is most likely his 'shadows aren't hitting it right'". Where a crest blocks the moon the sand is in shade: as dark as a slip face near the crest that throws it, lifting only a little with distance as the lit sand round it bounces light in. A cast shadow and the shaded flank that throws it are ONE dark shape.
3. ROOM FOR THE SHADOWS. The dunes are packed so tightly that their dark faces merge into one mass and there is almost no lit floor for a shadow's outline to lie on. Leave open, bright, rippled sand between the dunes (about a third of the desert), so each hollow and each long shadow has a hard curved edge against bright sand. That edge is what reads from across the room.
4. THE TREE LINE BEHIND THE DUNES, ruled by Fable and now in the plan (ROW in src/pieces/world/layout.ts, and the twelve trees in placed.ts at z -230, x 428 to 550, 16 to 25 units tall). Until tonight they stood off the land's rim and were never drawn. The picture has them as one even line of full round crowns, 1063 to 1174 across, crowns 227 to 243 down. Two things are yours:
   a. THE SHELF. The sand under the row stands at the height that puts each tree's foot at 244 down: fromPicture(px, 244, -230)[1] from the layout is that height at each place across. It comes to about 11 units over the dome at x 428, rising evenly to about 23 at x 556. A gentle swell, the foot of the pale dune behind them, never a platform or a terrace.
   b. THE PASS. The view never moves, so this is exact: no sand nearer the visitor than the row may be drawn higher than 246 down (ROW.clear), anywhere from 1055 to 1182 across. Test your crests with pictured() from the layout. That still leaves the front dunes there up to about 40 units tall (they are lower on the screen), the dunes left of 1055 and right of 1182 as tall as you like, and the pale dune BEHIND the row rising well over the crowns. The row then shows through a low pass between tall dunes, which is better than the picture.
   The trees are seated on your land as it is drawn by the trees' own script (scripts/world/parts/trees/seats.mjs reads your shape.mjs and your field): keep what it reads from you as it is, or say plainly in your report what changed. Do NOT run the trees' make script: the trees' builder is working this round. Prove a and b with numbers in your report (for each of the twelve: where its foot is drawn, and the highest sand in front of it along the line of sight). The dark groove across the pale back dune reads as a cut scoop: fix its face.
5. PLASTIC BACKS. Every windward back is an airbrushed gradient with nothing on it: at three times, plastic. Carry the ripples up the backs, fading toward the crest. Low secondary swells. Wind scours. Grain flow streaks down the slip faces. And color that varies: the crest palest, the flanks deeper, the floor between a little redder (the lit sand is one hue at saturation 0.51 everywhere, where his picture and picture 9 are 0.61 to 0.66).
6. THE RIPPLES are one wavelength and one amplitude over the whole floor, barely bend round a dune's toe, and turn to zebra stripes near shade. Their scale changes with slope and shelter, they bend round each dune, and there are no bright specks along crests that cross the trees.
7. THE RIM IS CAKE ICING. A vertical pale band with hanging drips and streaks round the planet's shoulder, and at the front a long straight black slot above a pale strip stippled with dither dots. Sand rolls over a rounded brink and lies as talus on the stone below, thinning out raggedly, with the skin's stone showing through it. No drips, no slot, no stipple. The skin's builder keeps his stone under your edge.
8. NO SEAM OF TWO MESHES where sand meets the grey stone strip at the city's edge (a ruler straight line, crazy paving blocks, a turned down slab front): run the sand to the lip or bury the strip unevenly.
