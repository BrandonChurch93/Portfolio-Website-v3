## Where it stands, told plainly
Round 1 scored 4 and 4.5. What it won, and you must not lose:
- One solid body. No flake, no air seen through, every mass with thickness and a backfill in shadow behind it.
- The outline on picture 9 to a few pixels: the roof's underside, the floor's top and underside, the torn edges each side, the core.
- Cracks cut as real grooves, the stone's quiet switch with no quilting or grain, one draw call, 3.43 MB, the live page untouched.
What it is, said harshly: A FLAT WALL OF CRAZY PAVING, LIT EVENLY. The plates round the ball all face the viewer in nearly one plane at the ball's own depth, so all of them are lit: a bright orange band 2.2 times as wide as it is tall, where the picture's lit rock is an oval 1.6 to 1, most of it mid tone, in a frame of black. There is no depth, no long shadow, and every face is flat with a net of grooves on it.

## The one idea of this round: the ball sits IN a socket
Picture 9's rock is a cave seen from its mouth. Build the depth that makes its light:
- THE BOWL IS BEHIND THE BALL. Straight behind the ball the back wall is about 1.2 R behind the ball's middle. It curls forward round it: where the rock is 1.3 R from the ball's middle on screen it is still 0.7 R behind it, at 1.9 R it is level with the ball's middle, and from there out it keeps coming toward the viewer.
- THE WALLS AND THE ROOF STAND IN FRONT. From about 2 R out, the masses at each side, the roof's front and the floor's front are NEARER THE VIEWER THAN THE BALL, by 0.5 R at 2.2 R and by 1 to 1.5 R at 3 R and beyond. So the faces they turn to the viewer are turned AWAY from the ball and are dark, and only their inner flanks, the sides that look back into the socket, are lit. That is why the picture's lit rock is an oval with a black frame: nobody painted the frame dark.
- So no mass outside 1.6 R may show the viewer a face lit square on. What the viewer sees lit out there is a flank seen at a slant (a lit stripe, wider toward the ball), a top that catches light from below, or a thin lit rim on an edge.
- LONG SHADOWS. The roof's lobes and the teeth at the right (picture 860 to 1000 across, 300 to 410 down) overhang the socket and stand between the ball and what is behind them: each throws a dark wedge across the mass behind it. The left books shade one another. The right half and the left books come to the picture's 50 to 60 percent black (41 and 39 now).
Do not get there by changing the light. LIGHT.core.fall stays as it is: at 2.5 to 3.5 R, S already has 2 percent lit where the picture has 9, so a steeper fall would kill the far lit rims the picture has. Get there by where the rock is and which way it faces.

## Then, in this order
1. FACES ARE STACKS, NOT SHEETS. Every great face is built of 3 to 8 overlapping layers, like shale or a stack of broken plates fanned out: each layer 3 to 10 units thick, its broken edge a lit lip with a shadowed step under it, edges that wander and chip, the layers of one mass running the same way (its bedding) and no two masses alike. About half of what was one flat lit face now turns away in steps. This is the picture's detail: look at the plates left of the ball in r9-0-picture-2x.jpg, seen nearly edge on. The grooves of round 1 stay only as a few fine fractures across the layers, one family to a face, a second that stops where it meets the first. No net, no diamonds, no three parallel planks, no Y branches repeated.
2. THE NUMBERS TO HIT (the reviewers' own, picture against round 1, in picture 9's pixels. Sample the same places and give the table):
   - Within 1.3 R of the ball's middle: lit pixels (luma over 120) 19 percent in the picture, 79 in S.
   - Mid tones overall in the hollow: 53 percent in the picture, 12 in S.
   - At 2.5 to 3.5 R: lit 9 percent in the picture, 2 in S.
   - Luma at places (picture, then S): far left board (315, 520) 149, 0. Back wall and book wedge (545, 470) 163, 32. The roof over the ball (620, 350) 214, 97. The right teeth (950, 360) 125, 9. The right shelf (900, 520) 190, 68. The floor's top (650, 532) 99, 203. The roof's front at the left (450, 320) is (37, 24, 10) in the picture and (101, 70, 53) in S: take the moon off it.
3. THE FLOOR. A narrow lit lip beside the ball, not a flat lit table: under it a mid brown front that undulates and steps back in terraces, broken by bedding that runs along it, with three or four hanging lobes on its underside where the small streams leave. Its rubble varies in size and shape and lies settled in the steps, not five dice on a shelf.
4. COLOR. The lit stone is rust: bright pixels are 0.56 green over red and 0.29 blue over red, where the picture's are 0.66 and 0.39. Bring the brightest rock toward tan and cream (still never paler than (245, 190, 130), never pink or grey), the mid tones a warm brown, in the stone or its bounce for sculpt S alone.
5. AT THREE TIMES every face is smooth clay with one even speckle, with less form per pixel than the picture or the old sculpt. Give the stone and the surface a second and third scale: pitting and small spalls of 1 to 3 units on the layers' lips, fine dust in the steps, hairline fractures. Never a pattern that repeats.
6. A straight grey rod, about (151, 120, 99), shows in a crack at the right at picture (928, 479). It is not there with only=rock, so something behind S is seen through a gap. Find whose it is, close the gap if it is S's, and say so if it is not.

## How to look
As round 1's brief has it, and first of all, before you change anything, look at the field from the side: a still or a plot of the rock cut through the ball's middle from above and from the side, with the ball drawn in, for round 1 and then for every draft. If the cut does not show a socket with the ball inside it and the walls standing in front, nothing else you do will make the picture's light.
Then squint at the side by side: is the bright shape an oval round the ball in a black frame, or a band?
The first image of your report is picture 9's hollow at the left and yours at the right. Under it, the same hard questions as round 1, and one more: is the black frame there because of where the rock stands?
