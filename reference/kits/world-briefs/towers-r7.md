## What must change, most important first
1. THE CITY DOES NOT GLOW. At the page's size it reads as "brown speckle", where the picture's reads as warm and lit, with windows in clumps. Three causes, all named by both reviewers:
   - the rooms are pale cream and clip: 78 percent of the city's bright pixels sit flat at the pale cap at saturation 0.24, where the picture's lit windows are AMBER (mid tones near 151, 105, 68, median saturation 0.33). A lit room runs from deep amber at its back to pale only at its lamp, and only a few rooms in a hundred reach the cap.
   - no tenant is larger than one window. Let some run three to five floors, a few a whole lit stair or lift core, a lit top floor, a dark block of floors between: clumps, never round 5's blotchy patches and never letters.
   - the walls are dark (their median is 80 against the picture's 99). Lit rooms throw a little warm light on the wall round them and on the street between the buildings' feet, so the city has a glow at its base.
2. ONE MOON LIGHTS IT, AND IT DOES NOT SHOW. Facades are lit evenly from roof to street (the same 48 / 102 / 170 everywhere). The moon is low at the visitor's upper right and behind (MOONWARD in layout.ts): put it on roof tops and parapets and on every face turned to it, let a tall tower's shadow fall across the lower ones beside it and SHOW at the page's own size, darken feet where buildings meet the ground and each other, and let the back rows fall off, so that from across the room the city has a lit side and a shaded side.
3. HIS STEPPED TOWER. Its crown is washed pale now: a third of its lit pixels sit near (251, 212, 156) at saturation 0.35, and its 90th percentile is 247 against the picture's 222. Hold the lit reeds near 220 with saturation over 0.5, the brightest thing a narrow glint, and clean the smeared mottling on each tier's end faces. Its limestone must be the limestone of the still he approved, lit piers (147, 135, 126), within 3 levels a channel, by measurement in the same place: a reviewer measured yours at (150, 139, 132), a little cooler, and beside the warmer city it now reads as white or silver. Do not warm it past the approved still either.
4. NEVER TWO ALIKE SIDE BY SIDE. The left end's front row is about nine narrow low blocks in two designs (a colonnade with a three window attic, flat or mansard) alternating at one width and one height. Vary design, height, attic and roofline from neighbour to neighbour.
5. THE DRUMS AND THE SAUCER are plain beside the stepped tower: vertical weather streaks that read as stretched texture or a seam, mullions that wobble, a flat stem grid and skirt. Build both as real buildings to the stepped tower's standard, not stacked rings. THE OBELISK's shaft is dim (its median 63 where the picture's is 123) and its point is plain: raise the shaft, keep its blaze on the moon's side (the picture's light there is not ours), and give the point a finish.
6. ONE BRICK MODULE on every stone and every building, about a metre tall, makes the city read as a model. Scale coursing to the floors and vary it from stone to stone.
7. DASHES. Thin bright edges on pilasters and window jambs break into dashes and small U and Y shaped fragments at three times: see common.md (04:20), widen them or fade them as they thin.
8. WEIGHT. 4.06 MiB: 4 MB or under.
