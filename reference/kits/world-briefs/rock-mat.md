Your material must work on whatever mesh that file holds: two meshes, `break` (the torn rock) and `skin` (the planet's outer surface, which stays plain and dark for now: another builder will make it), with the attributes normal, light (the share of the core seen, the share of the moon seen, how open, one spare) and bounce.

## What it is
The stone of the planet, seen in the tear: the roof under the land's lip, the slabs of the hollow lit by the core, the floor, the hanging masses. Later the same material is worn by every piece of what broke away, so write it as a function another part can call (export it from src/pieces/world/parts/rock/, with the maps it needs).

## What Fable sees in the picture's stone, and wants bettered
1. COLOR COMES FROM LIGHT. The stone is one warm stone. Where the core's light falls on it, it is a rich peach to deep orange tan. Where only bounced light reaches, a deep warm brown. Where nothing reaches, black. Under the moon (the top edge of the roof) it is a pale, cool leaning grey tan. Its own color must be chosen so that all four come out of the world's lights as they are (LIGHT in layout.ts, light.ts): a warm mid stone, not grey clay and not orange paint. Beds of slightly different stone (paler, redder, darker) lie through it in broad uneven bands tens of units thick: seen as a slow change of tone from slab to slab and across a great face, never as fine parallel lines (those read as wood grain and fail).
2. BROAD FACES ARE CLEAN, AND MARKED. A great face is smooth sheared stone with a soft sheen where it faces the core. On it, in places and never evenly: short dark gouges and fissures, 3 to 15 units long and a unit or two wide, lying mostly along the slab's own beds, in clusters, with a thin lit lip on the side toward the core. A few hairline cracks that run straight and turn sharply. Fine pitting. Then, at three times zoom, the grain of the stone itself. Nothing that reads as noise, as a net of cracks, or as a pattern that repeats.
3. EDGES ARE WHERE STONE BREAKS. Along every sharp edge the stone is chipped: paler, fresher, rougher, catching the light as a thin bright line where it faces the core. In creases and under overhangs it is darker and dusty. Use what the mesh tells you (the stored openness, the turn of the surface between pixels) to find edges and creases.
4. DUST ON WHAT FACES UP: ledges, the floor's top and the tops of slabs carry a paler, flatter dust and small grit.
5. THE CORE'S LIGHT IS HOT NEAR IT. Faces nearest the core go toward pale gold and stay stone: the surface is still seen in them.
6. IT IS ONE SCENE with the core and the stream (the kit, "One scene, one story"): the stream is a beam of light shot down from the core's foot (SOURCE in layout.ts). Its light lies on the rock near it: the floor's edge, the stubs under it, the inner faces of the hanging masses. Add that light in your material as a line of light from SOURCE straight down, warm, falling off within about 150 units, with a uniform for how strong it is that another part can set.

## How
A MeshStandardMaterial joined to the world's light with lit(m, { key, baked: true }) from src/pieces/world/light.ts (read it). The mesh has no texture coordinates: project your maps along the three axes, weighted by the normal, and give each slab its own shift and turn of the maps from a number that is steady across a face (derive one from the face's normal and place), so no repeat can be seen. The maps (relief as a normal and a height, a cavity, the gouges) are written by your script with scripts/world/lib/png.mjs, tile without a seam, and are mipmapped. Fade what is finer than a pixel. Budget: 2 shader programs (break and skin), 24 MB of textures, and it must hold the frame rate on a canvas the size of the screen: measure with npm run perf -- "/lab/piece/world?size=1520&wide=1460&label=0&ui=0".
