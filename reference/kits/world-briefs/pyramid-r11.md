## What must change, most important first
1. A FACE THAT SLOPES FIFTY DEGREES MIRRORS MOSTLY SKY. Work the reflected ray out for real, per pixel (the view never moves: the eye's direction, the face's normal with each sheet's small tilt, reflect). From this view the ray off the lit face leaves about 37 degrees UP: into the sky on the moon's side. So the lit face shows the sky's glow up most of its height, and land only in a strip near its foot, foreshortened by the slope, every bit of it tinted by the gold it is mirrored in. The level horizon across the middle of the face and the level dune bands are what make it read as "a dune picture printed on a plate" and "a sand painting behind glass". Each sheet shifts what it mirrors as a whole by its own tilt: no crest jogging strip by strip at every seam.
2. ONE BLAZE. On that sky the moon's glare is the event: one broad lobe, near pale at its heart, low and toward the right of the lit face (where the direction half way between the moon and the visitor meets the face's normal), falling smoothly to a deeper, richer, more saturated gold toward the apex and the right corner. From across the room the face is GOLD with a blaze on it, brighter than anything else on the first screen and a different tone from the dunes. Its median value now is 0.96 at saturation 0.53, even all over: the blaze's heart may be that, the rest of the face is deeper (value about 0.6 to 0.8, saturation over 0.65). No two pixel moon, no lozenge, no fleck, no halo: the glare is broad and soft edged, the sharpness comes from the seams breaking it.
3. THE DEEP FACE reads as chocolate at the page's size (saturation 0.80, hue 29, value 0.25). It mirrors the dark sky away from the moon and, low down, what stands before it. Give it one long soft gradient of dark gold down the slope (deep satin gold: saturation about 0.65, hue nearer 32, the same value), and at its foot the trees as soft uneven masses (the even row of bumps reads as a stencil). Take out the band of leaning stamps and window specks at mid face: it prints as a dotted line at the page's size. If the low city is mirrored at all it is a few soft upright dark masses near the foot with no windows.
4. THE CURSOR'S LIGHT is an upright pale pill that reads as one more lit window. It is the reflection of a lamp in a sloping mirror: a long thin sharp streak that lies ALONG the slope, slides as the cursor moves, breaks slightly at every seam, and falls off at its ends. It must read at the page's own size.
5. THE CAP. Its deep side is a flat brown plane with a wide soft caramel bevel, and its lit side repeats the face's desert as small wavy stripes. A narrow sharp bevel and seams of its own, its deep side the same dark satin gold as the face under it, its lit side the blaze's own gold a step brighter, and one hot glint at its tip: the brightest point of the pyramid.
