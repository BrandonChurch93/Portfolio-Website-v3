## What it is, as Fable has designed it
The page opens with a planet torn open and what broke away hanging under it, and the stream falls from its core the whole length of the page to the Sun stone under the rocket. Contact closes the page with the same rock: pieces of the planet that came down the stream's pull and hang in the air round the launch pad. It is the hero's field again, small and near, lit by the stone and by the rocket's fire. A visitor who has seen the hero knows it at a glance for the same stone.

One new part of the pad, `debris` (`src/pieces/pad/parts/debris.ts` and `parts/debris/`), behind the pad's contract.

- THE FIELD. 50 to 80 pieces in a loose, lopsided ring round the stone and the tower: more of it behind them and to the visitor's left, thinning toward the right, where the plate stands. Three or four great pieces (40 to 70 units across) low and far to the sides and behind, a dozen of middle size (12 to 25), and the rest small (3 to 8), down to slivers that could be the stream's own shards. Nothing regular: clusters and gaps, as the hero's field has. Pieces of a cluster move together.
- WHERE IT MAY NOT BE. Never in front of the rocket, the tower, the table or the stone's top as the page sees them (behind them and beside them, yes). Never in the stream's lane down to the stone (read where it lands: `src/contact/stage.ts`, `src/stream/route-contact.ts`, `STONE` in `layout.ts`), and never where the stream's thrown shards rest on the stone. Never over the plate or the title: read `src/contact/board.ts` for where they are against the pad's host, and keep the whole field inside the pad's own canvas with nothing cut by its edge. Within 70 units of the canvas's sides and top a piece has faded to nothing, so no rectangle can ever be seen.
- THE STONE. The planet's own: the same fifty rock models the hero's field wears (`public/world/debris.bin`, read through `src/pieces/world/files.ts`) and the same stone to the eye. How you get there is yours (the world's stone shader reads the world's own light, which the pad does not have: a material of the pad's own that lands on the same look is fine), but put a still of your pieces beside a still of the hero's at the same size and they are one family: the same red brown body, the same broken faces, the same fine grain. Never clay, never a faceted gem, never grey.
- THE LIGHT, which is the pad's and already there: the cone of light up from the stone's middle, which comes on with `fill`, the light at the engine, the cursor's light, the reflected room. Before the stream lands the field is dark shapes with a thin rim from the cursor's light, barely there: the section is asleep. As the stone fills, the pieces over and round it are lit from below, warm, nearest brightest. At the launch the engine's fire lights them hard from the middle and their far sides stay dark. They cast no shadow (the pad's only shadows are the cursor's, drawn only when something moved, and a field that never stops moving would draw them every frame), but they take the shadow of the tower and the rocket if that costs nothing.
- THE MOTION, all from the state and the clock alone (a held `t`, `fill` and `launch` is the same picture every time, and with `still` nothing moves). At rest every piece hangs and turns slowly with its own weight: a great piece barely moves, a sliver tumbles. At the launch the blast pushes the pieces near the engine outward and up, a little, the near ones first, and they come back as the fire dies. It reads as heavy rock in still air shoved by something powerful, never as confetti. Nothing loops in a way the eye can catch.
- THE COST. One draw call, one program, at most 60,000 triangles, no buffer written after it is made (the table of pieces goes to the card once as a texture, as the hero's does), nothing compiled or uploaded after the pad's first frame. The pad draws only when something changed: say plainly what the field does to that (it now moves all the time it is on the screen), and give the frame's time on `/lab/contact` and on `/lab/whole` at its foot before and after (`npm run perf`, on a quiet moment: other builders share the machine, so take it three times), with the processor slowed four times as well.

## The frame
`layout.ts`, `part.ts` and `index.ts` are the pad's frame. You may add `'debris'` to `PARTS` in `part.ts` and register the part in `index.ts` the way the others are, and add what the field's places need to `layout.ts` under one new constant. Change nothing else in them: say what you needed in your report. Do not touch the stone, the table, the tower or the rocket, the stream, `src/contact/`, or anything of the world's.
